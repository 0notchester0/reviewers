The Del ($\nabla$) Operator & 4 Primary Differential Forms

GRADIENT Scalar $\to$ Vector

Direction & rate of maximum steepest ascent.

$\nabla f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\hat{i} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\hat{j} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\hat{k}$
Normal vector to surface $f=C$: $\vec{N} = \nabla f$
DIVERGENCE Vector $\to$ Scalar

Net outward flux or expansion per unit volume.

$\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_z}{\partial z}$
If $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0 \implies$ Solenoidal (Incompressible)
CURL Vector $\to$ Vector

Rotational circulation / vorticity at a point.

$\nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ F_x & F_y & F_z \end{vmatrix}$
If $\nabla \times \vec{F} = 0 \implies$ Irrotational (Conservative)
LAPLACIAN Scalar $\to$ Scalar

Divergence of gradient; spatial curvature.

$\nabla^2 f = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial z^2}$
$\nabla^2 V = 0$ (Laplace) vs $\nabla^2 V = -\rho_v/\epsilon$ (Poisson)
IDENTITY 1: DIVERGENCE OF CURL dco [div curl = 0]
$$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{A}) \equiv 0$$

Physical Reason: A curl vector field purely represents vortices/circulation around an axis. Circulation loops are naturally continuous and closed; they cannot have a source or sink of expansion, so divergence is identically zero everywhere.

⚠️ Board Question 1: "Which of the following expressions will always result in a value of zero?" $\to$ $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{A})$
IDENTITY 2: CURL OF GRADIENT cgrado [curl grad = 0]
$$\nabla \times (\nabla V) \equiv \vec{0}$$

Physical Reason: A gradient vector field points strictly in the direction of maximum change of potential (conservative field). The line integral over any closed path in a conservative field is zero, meaning it cannot possess rotation or curl.

⚠️ Board Question 7: Given complex potential $V = \ln(x^2+y^2+z^2)$, find $\nabla \times \vec{E}$. Since $\vec{E} = -\nabla V$, $\nabla \times \vec{E} \equiv \vec{0}$ instantly!

Directional Derivative Formula

Q3 Master
$$D_{\vec{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \hat{u} = \nabla f \cdot \frac{\vec{A}}{|\vec{A}|}$$

Variables: $\nabla f$ = gradient evaluated at $P(x_0,y_0,z_0)$; $\vec{A}$ = given direction; $\hat{u}$ = normalized unit direction.

Use When: Asked for rate of change of scalar field in an arbitrary directional vector.

⚠️ Common Trap: Forgetting to divide $\vec{A}$ by magnitude $\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2+A_z^2}$.

Solved Exam Ex (Q3): $V = x^2 y - z^3$, $P(1,1,1)$, $\vec{A} = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$
1. $\nabla V = 2xy\hat{i} + x^2\hat{j} - 3z^2\hat{k} \implies \text{at } P: \langle 2, 1, -3 \rangle$
2. $|\vec{A}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2 + 2^2} = 3 \implies \hat{u} = \langle 2/3, -1/3, 2/3 \rangle$
3. $D_u V = 2(2/3) + 1(-1/3) - 3(2/3) = 4/3 - 1/3 - 6/3 = \mathbf{-1}$
Caltech: Vector mode $\to$ [VctA] $\cdot$ ([VctB] / Abs[VctB])

Tangent Plane to Implicit Surface

Q4 Master
$$\vec{N} = \nabla f = N_x\hat{i} + N_y\hat{j} + N_z\hat{k}$$ $$N_x(x - x_0) + N_y(y - y_0) + N_z(z - z_0) = 0$$

Variables: $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ = point of tangency; $\vec{N}$ = gradient normal vector.

Use When: Finding the exact plane tangent to a 3D curved surface.

⚠️ Common Trap: Forgetting to rewrite $z = g(x,y)$ into implicit form $f(x,y,z) = g(x,y) - z = 0$ to get $N_z = -1$.

Solved Exam Ex (Q4): $z = x^2 + 3y^2$ at $P(1,1,4)$
1. Implicit form: $f(x,y,z) = x^2 + 3y^2 - z = 0$
2. $\vec{N} = \nabla f = 2x\hat{i} + 6y\hat{j} - \hat{k} \implies \text{at } P(1,1,4): \vec{N} = 2\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} - \hat{k}$
3. $2(x - 1) + 6(y - 1) - 1(z - 4) = 0$
4. $2x - 2 + 6y - 6 - z + 4 = 0 \implies \mathbf{2x + 6y - z = 4}$

The Three Master Integral Theorems of Vector Calculus

Volume (3D) $\leftrightarrow$ Surface (2D)

Divergence Theorem (Gauss)

$$\iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV = \oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{S}$$

Connects net outward flux through closed surface $S$ to sum of all interior sources/sinks inside volume $V$.

EM Application: $\oiint \vec{D}\cdot d\vec{S} = \iiint \rho_v dV = Q_{\text{enc}}$
Surface (2D) $\leftrightarrow$ Line (1D)

Stokes' Theorem

$$\iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot d\vec{S} = \oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$$

Net surface circulation across an open curved surface equals the line circulation along its outer perimeter curve $C$.

EM Application: $\oint \vec{H}\cdot d\vec{l} = \iint \vec{J}\cdot d\vec{S} = I_{\text{enc}}$
Planar Area (2D) $\leftrightarrow$ Curve (1D)

Green's Theorem in the Plane

$$\oint_C (M\,dx + N\,dy) = \iint_R \left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right)dA$$

Planar version of Stokes' Theorem. The integrand is specifically the $\hat{k}$-component of the 2D curl!

Exam Trick (Q15): If $\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}$, integral $= 0$ regardless of area!

MAXWELL'S EQUATIONS MASTER MATRIX (STATIC & TIME-VARYING)

🔴 Mandatory Board Memorization
Law Name & Domain Differential Form Integral Form Physical Board Exam Principle
1st: Gauss's Law
Electrostatics
 $$\nabla \cdot \vec{D} = \rho_v$$ $$\oiint_S \vec{D}\cdot d\vec{S} = \iiint_V \rho_v dV = Q_{\text{enc}}$$ Electric flux lines originate on positive charge and terminate on negative charge.
2nd: Gauss's Law for Magnetism
Magnetostatics
 $$\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$$ $$\oiint_S \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{S} = 0$$ Isolated magnetic monopoles DO NOT EXIST. Magnetic flux lines always form continuous closed loops.
3rd: Faraday's Law
Electromagnetic Induction
 $$\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}$$ [Static: $\nabla \times \vec{E} = 0$] $$\oint_C \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l} = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt}\iint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{S}$$ Time-varying magnetic field produces a circulating, non-conservative electric field. Negative sign enforces Lenz's Law!
4th: Ampere-Maxwell Law
Magnetic Circuital
 $$\nabla \times \vec{H} = \vec{J} + \frac{\partial \vec{D}}{\partial t}$$ [Static: $\nabla \times \vec{H} = \vec{J}$] $$\oint_C \vec{H}\cdot d\vec{l} = \iint_S \left(\vec{J} + \frac{\partial \vec{D}}{\partial t}\right)\cdot d\vec{S} = I_{\text{total}}$$ Circulating magnetic field is generated by conduction current density $\vec{J}$ and displacement current density $\partial\vec{D}/\partial t$.

Parseval's Energy Theorem

Q19 & Q20
$$E = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |x(t)|^2\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |X(f)|^2\,df = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |X(\omega)|^2\,d\omega$$

Law of Conservation of Energy: The total energy computed by integrating instantaneous power in the time domain is identical to the area under the energy spectral density curve in frequency domain.

Decaying Exponential Energy Formula:
$$x(t) = A e^{-at} u(t) \implies E = \frac{A^2}{2a}\quad\text{[Joules]}$$
Exam Ex (Q20): $x(t) = 4e^{-3t}u(t) \implies A = 4, a = 3$
$$E = \frac{4^2}{2(3)} = \frac{16}{6} = \mathbf{\frac{8}{3}\text{ Joules}}$$

Improper Energy Integral & $p$-Series

Q21 Master
$$v(t) = \frac{1}{t^p}\quad (t \ge 1) \implies E = \int_1^{\infty} (v(t))^2\,dt = \int_1^{\infty} \frac{1}{t^{2p}}\,dt$$

The $p$-Integral Rule: $\int_1^{\infty} \frac{1}{t^k} dt$ converges to a finite value if and only if exponent $k > 1$.

Here, the squared voltage produces an exponent of $k = 2p$.

$$2p > 1 \implies \mathbf{p > 1/2}\quad\text{for Finite Energy}$$

⚠️ Common Trap: Answering $p > 1$. That applies to voltage itself $\int v(t)dt$, but energy requires squaring $v^2(t)$, resulting in $p > 1/2$!

Closed-Loop TF & Extracting $H(s)$

Q33 Master
$$T(s) = \frac{G(s)}{1 + G(s)H(s)}$$
$$H(s) = \frac{1}{T(s)} - \frac{1}{G(s)}$$

Use When: Given overall closed-loop transfer function $T(s)$ and forward path $G(s)$, solve for the primary feedback ratio $H(s)$.

Solved Exam Ex (Q33):
$G(s) = \frac{10}{s^2+4s}$, $T(s) = \frac{10}{s^2+4s+20}$
$$H(s) = \frac{s^2+4s+20}{10} - \frac{s^2+4s}{10} = \frac{20}{10} = \mathbf{2}$$

Sensitivity & Op-Amp Infinite Gain Limit

Q34 & Q35
$$S_A^T = \frac{\partial T / T}{\partial A / A} = \frac{1}{1 + AB}$$
Cause & Effect (Q34): Holding forward gain $A$ constant while increasing feedback gain $B$ causes sensitivity $S_A^T$ to DECREASE (system becomes far more stable against component variations).
Ideal Op-Amp Limit ($A \to \infty$) (Q35):
$$T = \frac{A}{1 + AB} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{A} + B} \xrightarrow{A \to \infty} \frac{1}{B}$$ If feedback factor $B$ is doubled $\implies$ closed loop gain is HALVED ($T_{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{2B} = 0.5 T_{\text{old}}$).

Mason's Gain Formula & $\Delta_k$

Q36 Master
$$T = \frac{1}{\Delta}\sum_k P_k \Delta_k$$
$\Delta$ (System Determinant):
$$\Delta = 1 - \sum L_1 + \sum L_2 - \sum L_3 + \dots$$ $\sum L_1$: sum of all individual loop gains.
$\sum L_2$: sum of gain products of 2 non-touching loops.
$\sum L_3$: sum of gain products of 3 non-touching loops.
🟠 What does $\Delta_k$ represent? (Exam Question):
The cofactor determinant of the graph evaluated with all loops touching the $k$-th forward path eliminated (set to 0).

Dominant Pole Identification

Q38

Given poles at $s = -1$, $s = -10$, $s = -100$:
• $s = -1 \implies e^{-t}$ (Decays very slowly)
• $s = -10 \implies e^{-10t}$ (Decays 10x faster)
• $s = -100 \implies e^{-100t}$ (Decays 100x faster, zeros out almost immediately)

🔴 Golden Rule: The Dominant Pole is the pole located closest to the imaginary ($j\omega$) axis because its transient mode decays the slowest!

Minimum vs. Non-Minimum Phase

Q39

Minimum Phase: All poles AND all zeros lie strictly in the Left-Half Plane (LHP) ($\text{Re} < 0$).

Non-Minimum Phase: Any system with at least one zero in the Right-Half Plane (RHP) ($\text{Re}(z) > 0$).

Exam Case (Q39): Transfer function with zero at $s = +3$ and poles at $s = -2 \pm j4$.
Because the zero is at $+3$ (RHP), this is classified as a Non-Minimum Phase System!

System Type, Static Error Constants & Steady-State Error ($e_{ss}$)

High-Yield Exam Matrix
⚠️ THE #1 BOARD EXAM TRAP: EXTRACTING SYSTEM TYPE FROM CLOSED-LOOP $T(s)$

NEVER read the System Type directly from the denominator of closed-loop $T(s)$! System Type is defined STRICTLY by the number of pure integrators ($s^N$ at the origin) in the OPEN-LOOP transfer function $G(s)H(s)$.

The Conversion Formula (Unity Feedback $H=1$):
$$G(s) = \frac{T(s)}{1 - T(s)} = \frac{\text{Numerator}(T)}{\text{Denominator}(T) - \text{Numerator}(T)}$$
Exam Problem Demo (Q41): Given $T(s) = \frac{10}{s^2 + 5s + 10}$
Trap intuition: "No lone $s$, so Type 0." (WRONG!)
Correct: $G(s) = \frac{10}{(s^2+5s+10) - 10} = \frac{10}{s^2+5s} = \frac{10}{\mathbf{s^1}(s+5)}$
Because of $s^1$, this is a TYPE 1 SYSTEM!
