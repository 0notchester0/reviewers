🔴 MUST MEMORIZE: Maxwell's 4 Equations & Fundamental Field Laws

Law Name Differential Form Integral / Circuit Form Physical Board Definition Exam Trap / Catch ⚠️
Gauss's Law for Electricity 1st Maxwell Equation \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{D} = \rho_v\) \(\oint_S \mathbf{D} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = Q_{\text{enc}}\) Net outward electric flux through any closed surface equals total enclosed free charge. Inside a solid conductor in static equilibrium, \(E = 0\) everywhere! (Q4)
Gauss's Law for Magnetism 2nd Maxwell Equation \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0\) \(\oint_S \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0\) No isolated magnetic monopoles exist in nature; magnetic field lines always form continuous closed loops. (Q5, Q11) Surface integral over any closed surface is strictly ZERO.
Faraday's Law of Induction 3rd Maxwell Equation \(\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}\) \(\text{emf} = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -L\frac{di}{dt}\) A time-varying magnetic field induces an electric field and EMF in a closed circuit. (Q12, Q13) Static fields induce ZERO EMF! Direction determined by Lenz's Law (opposes flux change).
Ampere's Circuital Law 4th Maxwell Equation \(\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t}\) \(\oint_C \mathbf{H} \cdot d\mathbf{L} = I_{\text{enc}} = \text{mmf}\) Conduction current and displacement current (\(\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t\)) generate circulating magnetic fields. For static DC current, \(\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t = 0\), leaving simple \(\oint \mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{L} = I\).
Biot-Savart Law Magnetostatic Source \(d\mathbf{H} = \frac{I \, d\mathbf{L} \times \hat{\mathbf{a}}_r}{4\pi R^2}\) \(\mathbf{H} = \int \frac{I \, d\mathbf{L}}{4\pi R^2}\) Calculates the magnetic field intensity generated by a steady DC current element. (Q3) Inverse-square law with distance \(R^2\); does NOT calculate electric fields!
Operator 1

Divergence

\(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = \frac{\partial A_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial A_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial A_z}{\partial z}\)

Physical Essence: Scalar representing the net outward flux per unit volume from an infinitesimal volume around a point.

Board Exam Clue: Source (+), Sink (-), or Incompressible (\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}=0\)). (Q10)
Operator 2

Curl

\(\nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \det\begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{i}} & \hat{\mathbf{j}} & \hat{\mathbf{k}} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \end{bmatrix}\)

Physical Essence: Vector representing the circulation or net rotation per unit area around an infinitesimal loop.

Board Exam Clue: "Rotational / Vortex field". If \(\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=0\), field is conservative.
Operator 3

Gradient

\(\nabla V = \frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\hat{\mathbf{i}} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y}\hat{\mathbf{j}} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\hat{\mathbf{k}}\)

Physical Essence: Vector pointing in the direction of the maximum spatial rate of increase (steepest ascent) of a scalar field.

Board Relation: \(\mathbf{E} = -\nabla V\) (Electric field points toward decreasing potential).
Operator 4

Laplacian

\(\nabla^2 V = \nabla \cdot (\nabla V) = \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial z^2}\)

Physical Essence: Divergence of the gradient. Represents the difference between potential at a point and average of surroundings.

Governing Equations: Laplace: \(\nabla^2 V = 0\); Poisson: \(\nabla^2 V = -\rho_v / \epsilon\).

⚡ Infinite Line Charge Electric Field Intensity

Q14 Core
\(E = \frac{\rho_L}{2\pi \epsilon_0 r} = \frac{2 k \rho_L}{r}\)
• \(\rho_L\): linear charge density \([\text{C/m}]\)
• \(r\): radial perpendicular distance \([\text{m}]\)
• \(\epsilon_0 \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m}\)
• \(k = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \approx 8.99 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2\)
⚠️ Common Board Trap: Do NOT square \(r\)! Coulomb's point charge is \(\propto 1/r^2\), but an infinite line charge is strictly \(\propto 1/r\)!

Magnetic Circuit Ohm's Law
\(\Phi = \frac{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{R}} \implies \mathcal{R} = \frac{N I}{\Phi} = \frac{l}{\mu_0 \mu_r A}\)

Reluctance \(\mathcal{R}\): Opposes setup of magnetic flux. Unit: \([\text{At/Wb}]\) or \([\text{H}^{-1}]\). Analogous to resistance \(R\), but does NOT consume continuous energy as heat.

Permeance: \(\mathcal{P} = 1/\mathcal{R}\) \([\text{Wb/At}]\) or \([\text{Henries}]\). (Q1, Q16)
Solenoid & Lorentz Force
\(H = \frac{NI}{l} \quad \text{and} \quad F = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}) = qvB\sin\theta\)

Field Intensity \(H\): Axial strength in solenoid \([\text{At/m}]\).
Lorentz Force: Force on charge \(q\) moving at velocity \(v\) through flux density \(B\).

⚠️ If charge is stationary (\(v=0\)) or \(\mathbf{v} \parallel \mathbf{B}\), \(F = 0\)! (Q15, Q17)
Coupled Inductor Configurations
Parallel-Aiding: \(L_{\text{eq}} = \frac{L_1 L_2 - M^2}{L_1 + L_2 - 2M}\)
Parallel-Opposing: \(L_{\text{eq}} = \frac{L_1 L_2 - M^2}{L_1 + L_2 + 2M}\)
Series: Aiding = \(L_1 + L_2 + 2M\); Opposing = \(L_1 + L_2 - 2M\). Notice numerator is identical for parallel! (Q29)

⚙️ Engineering Decision Tree: Preventing Magnetic Core Saturation

1. The Problem High DC bias current drives core into the nonlinear knee of the B-H curve (\(B_{\text{sat}}\)). \(\mu_r\) plunges rapidly, causing inductance to collapse.
2. The Solution (Q18) Introduce a precisely calculated air gap (\(l_g\)) into the magnetic circuit path.
3. Direct Effect (Q6) Total reluctance increases drastically:
\(\mathcal{R}_{\text{total}} = \frac{l_{\text{core}}}{\mu_r\mu_0 A} + \frac{l_{\text{gap}}}{\mu_0 A}\)
4. Net Engineering Result Inductance \(L = \frac{N^2}{\mathcal{R}}\) decreases, but remains linear and stable without saturating under fluctuating DC currents!

📈 LTI Systems & Transfer Function

Q31, Q32
\(H(s) = \mathcal{L}\{h(t)\} \Big|_{\text{zero initial conditions}} = \frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}\)

Transfer Function \(H(s)\): Laplace transform of impulse response \(h(t)\) under strictly zero initial conditions. (Q31)

Time-Invariance Principle: If \(x(t) \to y(t)\), then delayed input \(x(t - T) \to y(t - T)\). The output is delayed by the exact same time interval \(T\) without shape distortion or amplitude change. (Q32)

🎯 Final Value Theorem (FVT) Walkthrough

Q33
\(y(\infty) = \lim_{t \to \infty} y(t) = \lim_{s \to 0} s Y(s)\)

For input \(x(t) = u(t) \implies X(s) = \frac{1}{s}\):

\(Y(s) = H(s) \cdot \frac{1}{s} \implies y(\infty) = \lim_{s \to 0} s \left[H(s) \frac{1}{s}\right] = \lim_{s \to 0} H(s)\)

⚠️ FVT Condition: All poles of \(sY(s)\) must lie strictly in the open Left-Half of the s-plane (LHP). Invalid for unstable or oscillating systems!

Critical Distinction Points
Retentivity vs. Coercivity (Q2) B-H Curve
  • Retentivity (Remanence \(B_r\)): The ability of a magnetic core material to retain residual magnetism after the external magnetizing force \(H\) is turned off.
  • Coercivity (Coercive Force \(H_c\)): The reverse external magnetic field intensity required to completely reduce residual flux back to zero.
Ratio \(B/H\) vs. Ratio \(D/E\) (Q30) Constitutive Relations
  • \(\frac{B}{H} = \mu\) (Permeability): Unit is Henries per meter (\(\text{H/m}\)). Relates magnetic flux density to magnetic field intensity.
  • \(\frac{D}{E} = \epsilon\) (Permittivity): Unit is Farads per meter (\(\text{F/m}\)). Relates electric displacement to electric field intensity.
Air Gap Effects on Core (Q6, Q18) Inductance vs. Reluctance
  • Adding an air gap INCREASES total reluctance \(\mathcal{R}\) (\(\mathcal{R}_{\text{gap}} = \frac{l_g}{\mu_0 A}\) is huge because \(\mu_0 \ll \mu_{\text{core}}\)).
  • Since \(L = \frac{N^2}{\mathcal{R}}\), adding an air gap DECREASES self-inductance \(L\).
  • Purpose: Extends the saturation threshold under heavy DC bias!
Phase Lead/Lag: ELI the ICE man (Q25) Passive AC
  • ELI: In an Inductor (\(L\)), Voltage (\(E\)) leads Current (\(I\)) by \(90^\circ\).
  • ICE: In a Capacitor (\(C\)), Current (\(I\)) leads Voltage (\(E\)) by \(90^\circ\).
