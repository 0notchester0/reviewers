MUST MEMORIZE Interatomic Bond Predictor: $\Delta EN$

[NOTES Q1, Q2]
Difference in Electronegativities ($\Delta EN = |EN_A - EN_B|$)
$\Delta EN > 1.7 - 2.0$ IONIC BOND Valence electron TRANSFER from cation to anion. (Ceramics, salts, oxides)
VS
$\Delta EN < 1.7$ COVALENT BOND Valence electron SHARING. Highly directional; stable electronic octet.
Hannay-Smyth % Ionic Character Equation: [SUPPLEMENTAL]
$$\% \text{ Ionic Character} = \left( 1 - e^{-(0.25)(\Delta EN)^2} \right) \times 100\%$$

When $\Delta EN = 1.7$, the bond is exactly $50\%$ ionic and $50\%$ covalent. Greater $\Delta EN$ yields predominantly ionic ceramic character.

AXIAL STRESS & HOOKE'S LAW [NOTES Q25, Q26]
$$\sigma = \frac{F}{A_0}, \quad \varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0}, \quad \sigma = E\varepsilon \implies \Delta L = \frac{F \cdot L_0}{A_0 \cdot E}$$

• $\sigma$: Engineering Stress $[\text{Pa or N/m}^2]$ (Force / original area $A_0$).

• $E$: Young's Modulus of Elasticity $[\text{Pa}]$ (slope of linear elastic region).

• $A_0$: Original cross-sectional area $= \frac{\pi}{4}d_0^2$ $[\text{m}^2]$.

⚠️ TRAP: Always use original undeformed area $A_0$, never the instantaneous necked area.

POISSON'S RATIO & LATERAL STRAIN [NOTES Q28, Q33]
$$\nu = -\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{lateral}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{axial}}} = -\frac{\Delta d / d_0}{\Delta L / L_0} \implies |\Delta d| = \nu \cdot d_0 \cdot \left(\frac{\sigma}{E}\right)$$

• $\nu$: Poisson's ratio (dimensionless; typically $0.25 - 0.35$ for metals).

• $\varepsilon_{\text{lateral}}$: Transverse strain (contraction under tension $\implies$ negative).

• $|\Delta d|$: Decrease in conductor diameter $[\text{m or mm}]$.

⚠️ TRAP: When $E$ is in $\text{GPa}$, multiply by $10^9$ before calculating strain!

Step-by-Step Board Exam Computation Walkthroughs

Problem 1: Unit Cells in Iron [Q22]

$5.00\text{ g}$ of pure $\alpha$-Fe (BCC, $A = 55.85\text{ g/mol}$). Find number of unit cells.

$N_{\text{atoms}} = \frac{m}{A} N_A$
$N_{\text{atoms}} = \frac{5.00}{55.85}(6.022 \times 10^{23}) = 5.391 \times 10^{22}$
$N_{\text{cells}} = \frac{N_{\text{atoms}}}{2} = \mathbf{2.6955 \times 10^{22}\ \text{cells}}$
✓ Rule: BCC has 2 atoms/cell $\implies$ divide total atoms by 2!
Problem 2: Guy Wire Elongation [Q32]

Steel wire: $L = 40\text{ m}$, $d = 8\text{ mm}$, $F = 2500\text{ N}$, $E = 200\text{ GPa}$. Find $\Delta L$.

$A_0 = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.008\text{ m})^2 = 5.0265 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$
$\Delta L = \frac{(2500\text{ N})(40\text{ m})}{(5.0265 \times 10^{-5})(200 \times 10^9)}$
$\Delta L = 9.947 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} \approx \mathbf{9.95\ \text{mm}\ (1.0\ \text{cm})}$
✓ Rule: Always convert diameter to meters before calculating area.
Problem 3: Coax Lateral Shrinkage [Q33]

Cu wire: $F = 500\text{ N}$, $d_0 = 8\text{ mm}$, $E = 110\text{ GPa}$, $\nu = 0.33$. Find $|\Delta d|$.

$\sigma = \frac{500\text{ N}}{5.0265 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2} = 9.947\text{ MPa}$
$\varepsilon_{\text{axial}} = \frac{9.947\text{ MPa}}{110\text{ GPa}} = 9.043 \times 10^{-5}$
$|\Delta d| = (0.33)(8\text{ mm})(9.043 \times 10^{-5}) = \mathbf{2.39 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{mm}}$
✓ Result: $|\Delta d| = 0.239\ \mu\text{m}$. Dielectric is intact!

Polymers, Advanced Ceramics & Heat Treatment Cycles

Critical dielectric properties, RF substrate selections, cooling cycle pathways (martensite vs pearlite), and optical-magnetic phenomena.

Essential ECE Polymers [NOTES]

Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC) [NOTES Q7]

Standard outer jacket for indoor house wiring and twisted-pair Ethernet (Cat5e/Cat6). Chosen for low cost, flexibility, and inherent flame retardancy.

Polyimide (PI / Kapton) [NOTES Slides]

Distinct translucent amber/yellow color. The undisputed industry standard for Flexible Printed Circuits (FPCs) due to extreme thermal stability.

Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE / Teflon) [NOTES Slides]

Ultra-low dielectric constant ($\varepsilon_r \approx 2.1$) and lowest dissipation factor ($\tan \delta$). Ideal inner dielectric for high-frequency RF coaxial cables and microwave PCBs.

Polyethylene (PE) [NOTES Slides]

High dielectric breakdown strength, water impervious. Standard primary insulation for high-voltage underground & submarine power transmission cables.

Essential ECE Ceramics [NOTES]

Barium Titanate ($BaTiO_3$) [NOTES Slides, Q44]

Massive dielectric constant ($\varepsilon_r > 1000$ to $10,000$). Primary material for Multi-Layer Ceramic Capacitors (MLCCs); strong reversible piezoelectric effect.

Alumina (Aluminum Oxide, $Al_2O_3$) [NOTES Slides]

Hard, high-wear resistance, high dielectric strength. Standard substrate for thick-film hybrid resistors, power modules, and IC packaging.

Aluminum Nitride ($AlN$) & Beryllium Oxide ($BeO$) [NOTES Slides]

Metal-grade thermal conductivities ($150 - 260\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) while remaining strict electrical insulators. High-power RF/microwave transistor heat sinks.

Refractories & Titanium Dioxide ($TiO_2$) [NOTES Q11]

Refractories: Ceramics engineered to endure temperatures $> 1500^\circ\text{C}$ without melting. $TiO_2$: used in low-loss, temperature-compensating RF capacitors.

Steel Heat Treatment Spectrum & Phase Control [NOTES]

MUST MASTER
1. Quenching

Rapid cooling of red-hot metal from above its critical temperature into water, brine, or oil.

Result: Freezes carbon into distorted Martensite. Produces maximum hardness but extreme brittleness.
2. Tempering

Gently reheating quenched steel to a low sub-critical range ($200^\circ\text{C} - 500^\circ\text{C}$).

Result: Relieves internal quenching stresses. Trades a fraction of hardness to significantly restore toughness and ductility.
3. Normalizing

Heating above critical range and cooling in still, room-temperature air.

Result: Intermediate cooling speed. Refines grain structure, producing uniform pearlite with balanced strength and machinability.
4. Annealing

Heating workpiece and letting it cool extremely slowly inside the turned-off furnace overnight.

Result: Complete stress relief; coarse pearlite. Produces maximum ductility, softness, and lowest hardness.
OPTICAL PHENOMENA & BALANCE: $T + R + A = 1$ [NOTES Q41-Q43]
Transparent: All light passes directly; high image resolution.
Translucent: Diffuse transmission; internal light scattering.
Opaque: No light transmitted; $100\%$ absorbed/reflected.
Fluorescence vs Phosphorescence:

Fluorescence: Absorbs UV, immediately re-emits visible light; stops within $<10^{-8}\text{ s}$ of cutoff.
Phosphorescence: Has a built-in time delay (glow-in-the-dark) due to forbidden energy states.

MAGNETIC TAXONOMY & CURIE TEMPERATURE [NOTES Q38-Q40]
Diamagnetic: Weakly repelled by magnetic fields ($\mu_r < 1$).
Cu, Au, Ag, Bi
Paramagnetic: Weakly attracted ($\mu_r > 1$); loses alignment.
Al, Ti, Pt
Ferromagnetic: Spontaneous domain alignment; strong attraction.
Fe, Co, Ni
Curie Temperature ($T_c$): Thermal threshold above which spontaneous domain alignment is lost $\implies$ ferromagnetic becomes paramagnetic!

CREEP vs FATIGUE [Q30]
CREEP: Slow, permanent plastic deformation under constant, static stress over prolonged time; accelerated by high heat.
FATIGUE: Catastrophic fracture under fluctuating, cyclic, or dynamic loads at stresses well below the yield strength.
RESILIENCE vs TOUGHNESS
RESILIENCE [Q27]: Capacity to absorb energy when deformed elastically and fully release it upon unloading (area under elastic region).
TOUGHNESS: Total energy absorbed prior to fracture (area under entire $\sigma$-$\varepsilon$ curve, elastic + plastic).
SOLID SOLUTIONS [Q16, Q36]
INTERSTITIAL: Solute atoms are small enough to sit in the interstitial voids/spaces between host atoms (e.g. Carbon in Steel).
SUBSTITUTIONAL: Solute atoms of comparable size replace solvent atoms directly on lattice sites (e.g. Zinc in Brass [Q37]).
