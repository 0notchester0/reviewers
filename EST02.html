SIGNALS, SYSTEM PROPERTIES & NYQUIST SAMPLING

🔴 HIGH CALCULATION FREQUENCY

⚡ Energy vs. Power Signal Decision Engine

🔴 [MUST MEMORIZE]
💡 STUDENT BOARD MNEMONIC [NOTES]:

"Kung anong signal siya, yun yung finite sa kanya!"

(If Energy signal $\to$ Energy is finite. If Power signal $\to$ Power is finite.)

E
Energy Signal
Finite Total Energy ($0 < E < \infty$), Zero Average Power ($P = 0$)
Examples: Single rectangular pulse, decaying exponential $e^{-\alpha t}u(t)$, unit impulse $\delta(t)$.
P
Power Signal
Finite Average Power ($0 < P < \infty$), Infinite Energy ($E = \infty$)
Examples: Sustained pure sine wave $A\sin(\omega t)$, periodic square wave, sustained random noise, constant DC.
⚠️
Neither Energy Nor Power Signal
Energy = $\infty$, Average Power = $\infty$ (Signal explodes or grows unboundedly)
Examples: Unit ramp function $r(t) = t \cdot u(t)$, growing exponential $e^{+at}$.

⚙️ Linearity & Causality Verification

🟠 [HIGH-YIELD]
Principle of Superposition

1. Additivity: $T\{x_1 + x_2\} = T\{x_1\} + T\{x_2\}$

2. Homogeneity (Scaling): $T\{a \cdot x\} = a \cdot T\{x\}$

Causality Analogies [NOTES]

Causal: Depends on present / past inputs only. "Straight payment / Cash-on-hand"

Non-Causal: Depends on future inputs ($t > t_0$). "Home Credit / Post-dated check"

⚠️ BOARD EXAM TRAP CASE STUDY [QUESTION 12]:

Given system: $y(t) = x(\sin(t))$

• Test Causality at $t = -\frac{\pi}{2} \approx -1.57\text{ s}$:

$$y(-1.57) = x(\sin(-1.57)) = x(-1)$$

• Since $-1 > -1.57$, the system requires an input from the FUTURE! $\implies$ NON-CAUSAL.

• Linearity Check: $T\{a x_1 + b x_2\} = a x_1(\sin t) + b x_2(\sin t)$ satisfies superposition $\implies$ LINEAR.

Final Result: The system is LINEAR and NON-CAUSAL.

TIME SCALING $x(at)$

$a > 1$ (e.g. $x(2t)$): Time compression (factor of $a$). Downsampling analogy.

$0 < a < 1$ (e.g. $x(t/2)$): Time expansion / stretching (factor $1/a$). Upsampling analogy.

POLARITY DEFINITIONS

Unipolar: Amplitude $\ge 0$ at all times (never drops below zero ground).

Bipolar: Alternates between positive and negative voltage rails.

NYQUIST SAMPLING THEOREM & ALIASING FOLD-OVER

🔴 [MUST MEMORIZE]
Nyquist Rate ($f_{s,\min}$)
$$f_s \ge 2 \cdot f_{\max}$$

Minimum sampling frequency to prevent spectrum overlap.

Nyquist Frequency ($f_{\text{Nyquist}}$)
$$f_{\text{Nyquist}} = \frac{f_s}{2}$$

Maximum signal frequency faithfully captured by rate $f_s$.

Principal Alias Frequency ($f_{\text{alias}}$)
$$f_{\text{alias}} = |f_{\text{sig}} - k \cdot f_s|$$

Where $k \cdot f_s$ is the nearest integer harmonic multiple.

WORKED EXAMPLE A [QUESTION 13]: NYQUIST RATE

Given: $x(t) = 3\cos(2000\pi t) + 5\sin(6000\pi t)$

  1. Extract component frequencies:
      $\omega_1 = 2000\pi \implies f_1 = \frac{2000\pi}{2\pi} = 1000\text{ Hz}$
      $\omega_2 = 6000\pi \implies f_2 = \frac{6000\pi}{2\pi} = 3000\text{ Hz}$
  2. Highest frequency present: $f_{\max} = 3000\text{ Hz}$
  3. Nyquist Rate: $f_N = 2 \cdot f_{\max} = 2 \cdot (3000) = \mathbf{6000\text{ Hz}}$
WORKED EXAMPLE B [QUESTION 14]: ALIASING CALCULATION

Given: $f_{\text{sig}} = 7\text{ kHz}$, sampled at rate $f_s = 10\text{ kHz}$

  1. Check Nyquist condition: Required $f_s \ge 2(7) = 14\text{ kHz}$.
  2. Actual rate $10\text{ kHz} < 14\text{ kHz} \implies$ Aliasing occurs!
  3. Nearest multiple of $f_s$ to $7\text{ kHz}$ is $10\text{ kHz}$ ($k=1$).
  4. Alias frequency:
      $f_{\text{alias}} = |7\text{ kHz} - 10\text{ kHz}| = \mathbf{3\text{ kHz}}$
Oversampling [NOTES]: Sampling a $5\text{ kHz}$ signal at $100\text{ kHz}$ ($f_s \gg 2f_{\max}$) relaxes anti-aliasing analog filter design requirements. $f_s \gg 2 f_{\max}$

DIGITAL SIGNAL PROCESSING, FILTERS & Z-TRANSFORMS

🟠 CORE FILTER THEORY

🎛️ Master FIR vs. IIR Filter Taxonomy [NOTES]

🔴 [MUST MEMORIZE]
Feature / Metric FIR (Finite Impulse Response) IIR (Infinite Impulse Response) Board Exam Significance
Topology Feedforward only (Non-recursive) Feedback present (Recursive) "Kapag may past $y[n-k]$, IIR yan!"
Phase Response Strictly Linear Phase (Perfect Timing) Non-linear Phase (Phase distortion occurs) FIR is mandatory for audio/biomedical where shape matters
Transfer Function $H(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{M} a_k z^{-k}$ (All-Zero) $H(z) = \frac{\sum a_k z^{-k}}{1 + \sum b_k z^{-k}}$ (Pole-Zero) IIR denominator contains feedback coefficients
System Stability Inherently Stable (Poles at origin $z=0$) Conditionally Stable (Poles must remain in unit circle) Rounding coefficients can push IIR poles outside $|z|=1$
Order Efficiency Requires higher order (Many taps / more math) Achieves sharp cutoff with few taps / less math IIR is preferred for low-memory microcontrollers
Analog Conversion Windowing / Frequency Sampling Bilinear Transform / Impulse Invariance Question 24 board answer: "Bilinear Transform"

📏 FIR Filter Order vs. Tap Length [NOTES]

$$L = n + 1$$

Order ($n$): Defined strictly by the maximum delay index in the difference equation ($z^{-n}$).
Length ($L$): Total number of impulse response samples/taps ($L = n + 1$).

Order = 4 Length = 5
Question 21 $y[n] = x[n-2] + 3x[n-7]$
Order = 7 Length = 8
Question 21 Example 2 $y[n] = \sum_{k=2}^{6} k \cdot x[n-k]$
Order = 6 Length = 7
CASCADED DIFFERENCE EQUATION [QUESTION 22]:

Filter 1: $w[n] = x[n] + 0.5x[n-1]$

Filter 2: $y[n] = w[n] - 0.2w[n-1]$

$$\implies y[n] = x[n] + 0.3x[n-1] - 0.1x[n-2]$$

Result: 2nd Order FIR Filter, Tap Length = 3

✖️ Linear Convolution & Parseval's Law

$$L_y = L_x + L_h - 1$$
CONVOLUTION THEOREM

Convolution in time is point-wise multiplication in frequency:

$$x(t) * h(t) \iff X(f) \cdot H(f)$$

PARSEVAL'S THEOREM

Total energy in time equals total energy in frequency (Conservation):

$$\int |x(t)|^2 dt = \int |X(f)|^2 df$$

DISCRETE CONVOLUTION MATRIX METHOD [QUESTION 27]:

Convolve $x[n] = \{1, 2\}$ ($L_x=2$) with $h[n] = \{1, -1\}$ ($L_h=2$):

*12
112
-1-1-2

Diagonal 1: $y[0] = 1$

Diagonal 2: $y[1] = 2 + (-1) = \mathbf{1}$

Diagonal 3: $y[2] = \mathbf{-2}$

Result: $y[n] = \{1, 1, -2\}$

Auto-Correlation [NOTES]: Measures similarity of a signal with a time-delayed version of itself. Primary application: Detecting periodic signals heavily corrupted by noise.
Z-PLANE ENGINE

Z-PLANE POLE-ZERO FREQUENCY ENGINE & ROC STABILITY

🔴 [MUST MEMORIZE]
1. STABILITY & ROC RULES

• BIBO Stable: Region of Convergence (ROC) MUST INCLUDE THE UNIT CIRCLE ($|z| = 1$).

• Causal + Stable: All poles must reside strictly INSIDE the unit circle: $|p_k| < 1$.

• ROC Direction:

Causal: Outward from outermost pole ($|z| > r_{\max}$) Anti-Causal: Inward from innermost pole ($|z| < r_{\min}$)

• Minimum Phase System [NOTES]: ALL poles AND ALL zeros lie strictly inside the unit circle.

2. POLES VS. ZEROS DYNAMICS
POLES ($\times$) $\longrightarrow$ BOOST Frequencies ZEROS ($\circ$) $\longrightarrow$ BLOCK Frequencies

Unit Circle Angle Mapping:

  • $\omega = 0 \iff z = 1$ (DC / Low Frequency)
  • $\omega = \pi \iff z = -1$ (Nyquist Frequency $f_s/2$)
Unit Circle Zero Blocking Rules [NOTES]:
• Zero at $z = +1 \implies$ Completely blocks DC (0 Hz)
• Zero at $z = -1 \implies$ Completely blocks Nyquist ($f_s/2$)
3. BOARD EXAM CASE STUDIES
CASE STUDY 1 [QUESTION 34]:

$$H(z) = \frac{z - 0.5}{z^2 + 0.25}$$

Poles: $z^2 + 0.25 = 0 \implies z = \pm j0.5$

Magnitude: $|z| = \mathbf{0.5} < 1 \implies$ STABLE.

CASE STUDY 2 [QUESTION 37]:

Given: Pole at $z = 0.9$, Zero at $z = -1$.

• Pole at $z=0.9$ boosts low freq ($\omega=0$).

• Zero at $z=-1$ blocks high freq ($\omega=\pi$).

$\implies$ LOW-PASS FILTER (LPF)

COMMUNICATIONS NOISE & RECEIVER SENSITIVITY

🔴 CRITICAL RF CALCULATIONS

INTERNAL NOISE (Device Generated)

• Thermal Noise (Johnson-Nyquist): Random thermal agitation of electrons inside conductors. White noise spectrum (flat power spectral density across all frequencies).

• Shot Noise (Poisson / Schottky): Discrete arrival/emission of carriers across potential barriers / pn junctions in semiconductor devices.

• Flicker Noise ($1/f$): Dominates at low frequencies ($< 1\text{ kHz}$) due to surface state traps.

• Transit-Time Noise: High-frequency carriers taking finite duration to cross device channels.

EXTERNAL NOISE (Environment)

• Atmospheric Noise [Question 49]: Naturally occurring electrical static disturbances from lightning discharges. Dominates below $30\text{ MHz}$.

• Extraterrestrial Noise: Solar noise (radiation from sun) and Cosmic noise (deep space star emission).

• Industrial / Man-Made Noise: Ignition sparks, electric motors, switching power supplies.

THERMAL NOISE, NOISE FACTOR & FRIIS' CASCADE

🔴 [MUST MEMORIZE]
Available Noise Power
$$P_n = k \cdot T \cdot B$$
[Watts]
RMS Noise Voltage
$$V_n = \sqrt{4 k T B R}$$
[Volts RMS]
Equivalent Noise Temp
$$T_e = T_0(F - 1)$$
$T_0 = 290\text{ K}$
Friis' Cascaded Formula
$$F_T = F_1 + \frac{F_2 - 1}{G_1} + \frac{F_3 - 1}{G_1 G_2}$$
ALL LINEAR RATIOS!
⚠️ HIGH-RISK COMPUTATIONAL TRAPS & NOTE ERRATA CORRECTION:

• Conversion Trap: In Friis' Formula, $F_n$ and $G_n$ MUST be LINEAR RATIOS, never decibels!

$G_{\text{linear}} = 10^{G_{\text{dB}}/10}, \quad F_{\text{linear}} = 10^{NF_{\text{dB}}/10}$

• ERRATA IN HANDWRITTEN NOTES [QUESTION 40]:

Given $T=290\text{ K}, B=10\text{ MHz} \implies P_n = 4.004 \times 10^{-14}\text{ W}$.

$P_n(\text{dBm}) = 10\log_{10}(P_n / 1\text{ mW}) = \mathbf{-103.98\text{ dBm}}$ (Notes recorded $-107.98$).

NOISE FIGURE CALC [Q41]

$\text{SNR}_{\text{in}} = 45\text{ dB}$, $\text{SNR}_{\text{out}} = 38\text{ dB}$

$$NF = 45\text{ dB} - 38\text{ dB} = \mathbf{7\text{ dB}}$$

Ideal noiseless device: $F = 1, NF = 0\text{ dB}$.

EQUIV NOISE TEMP [Q42]

$NF = 6\text{ dB} \implies F = 10^{0.6} \approx 3.981$

$$T_e = 290(3.981 - 1)$$

$$T_e = \mathbf{864.51\text{ K}}$$

FRIIS' CASCADE [Q43]

$G_1 = 10\text{ dB} \implies G_1 = 10$; $F_1 = 2$; $F_2 = 5$

$$F_T = 2 + \frac{5 - 1}{10} = 2 + 0.4$$

$$F_T = \mathbf{2.4} \quad (\approx 3.80\text{ dB})$$

AM TRANSMISSION POWER, ANTENNA CURRENT & SAVINGS

🔴 [MUST MEMORIZE]
Modulation Index ($m$)
$$m = \frac{V_m}{V_c} = \frac{V_{\max} - V_{\min}}{V_{\max} + V_{\min}}$$
Total Transmitted Power
$$P_T = P_c \left(1 + \frac{m^2}{2}\right)$$
Total Antenna Current
$$I_T = I_c \sqrt{1 + \frac{m^2}{2}}$$
TOTAL POWER CALC [Q52]

Given: $P_c = 400\text{ W}$, $m = 0.8$ ($80\%$)

$$P_T = 400\left(1 + \frac{0.8^2}{2}\right) = 400(1.32)$$

$$P_T = \mathbf{528\text{ W}}$$

CURRENT TO MODULATION [Q53]

Given: $I_c = 10\text{ A}$, $I_T = 10.86\text{ A}$

$$\frac{10.86}{10} = \sqrt{1 + \frac{m^2}{2}} \implies 1.1794 = 1 + \frac{m^2}{2}$$

$$m = \sqrt{0.3588} \approx \mathbf{0.6 \ (60\%)}$$

MODULATION HIKE [Q54]

Modulation changes from $m = 0 \longrightarrow m = 1$:

$$P_T(0) = P_c \longrightarrow P_T(1) = 1.5 P_c$$

Transmitted Power Increases by 50%!

⚡ POWER DISTRIBUTION & SSB-SC POWER SAVINGS BREAKDOWN [QUESTION 55] 83.33% SAVED
Total Power ($m=1$) $P_T = 1.5 P_c$ 100% of Total
Carrier Power ($P_c$) $\frac{P_c}{1.5 P_c} = \mathbf{66.67\%}$ ($2/3$ of total power)
Both Sidebands ($P_{SB}$) $\frac{0.5 P_c}{1.5 P_c} = \mathbf{33.33\%}$ ($1/3$ of total power)
One Sideband Only ($P_{USB}$) $\frac{0.25 P_c}{1.5 P_c} = \mathbf{16.67\%}$ ($1/6$ of total power)

• In SSB-SC ($J3E$), the carrier and one sideband are suppressed. Transmitting only 1 sideband requires $\frac{1}{6}$ power $\implies \mathbf{100\% - 16.67\% = 83.33\%\ (5/6)\ \text{SAVED}}$!
• If upgrading to DSB-SC (suppressing carrier only): Saves $66.67\%\ (2/3)$ of total power.

⚠️ NYQUIST RATE VS. NYQUIST FREQUENCY
Nyquist Rate $$f_N = 2 \cdot f_{\max}$$ Min sampling speed to prevent aliasing.
Nyquist Frequency $$f_{\text{Nyq}} = f_s / 2$$ Highest analog freq safely recorded.
⚠️ FIR ORDER ($n$) VS. TAP LENGTH ($L$)
Order ($n$) Maximum delay index E.g., $x[n-4] \implies n = 4$.
Length ($L$) $$L = n + 1$$ Always 1 greater than the order!
⚠️ Z-PLANE ZEROES VS. POLES
Poles ($\times$) BOOST frequencies Pull transfer function to $\infty$.
Zeros ($\circ$) BLOCK frequencies $z=1$ blocks DC; $z=-1$ blocks Nyquist.
⚠️ NOISE FACTOR ($F$) VS. NOISE FIGURE ($NF$)
Noise Factor ($F$) Linear Ratio $\ge 1$ Ideal noiseless $= 1.0$.
Noise Figure ($NF$) $$NF = 10\log_{10}(F)$$ Ideal noiseless $= 0\text{ dB}$.
