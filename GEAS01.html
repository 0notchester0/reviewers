🚗 MODULE 1: KINEMATICS (1D, 2D, PROJECTILE & ANGULAR)

🔴 ESSENTIAL

1D Linear Kinematics (Constant Acceleration)

  • • $v = v_0 + a t$
  • • $x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2$
  • • $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2 a (x - x_0)$
  • • $\Delta x = \left(\frac{v_0 + v}{2}\right) t$

Free Fall Rules: $a = -g = -9.81\text{ m/s}^2$ (or $-32.2\text{ ft/s}^2$). Time to reach peak $t_{up} = v_0/g$. Maximum height $H_{max} = \frac{v_0^2}{2g}$. Total flight time $T_{flight} = 2 t_{up}$.

2D Projectile Motion Equations

HORIZONTAL ($a_x = 0$) $v_x = v_0 \cos\theta$
$x = (v_0 \cos\theta) t$
VERTICAL ($a_y = -g$) $v_y = v_0 \sin\theta - g t$
$y = (v_0 \sin\theta) t - \frac{1}{2}g t^2$
• Time of Flight: $T = \frac{2 v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$
• Max Height: $H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$
• Horizontal Range: $R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$ (Max at $\theta = 45^\circ$)
• Trajectory: $y = x\tan\theta - \frac{g x^2}{2 v_0^2 \cos^2\theta}$
• When $H = R \implies \tan\theta = 4 \implies \theta = 75.96^\circ$

Linear vs. Angular Kinematics Analogy Table

Linear Quantity Angular Quantity Bridging Relationship Kinematic Equation Equivalent
Displacement $x$ [m] Angle $\theta$ [rad] $s = r \theta$ $\theta = \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$
Velocity $v$ [m/s] Angular Speed $\omega$ [rad/s] $v = r \omega$ $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t$
Acceleration $a$ [m/s²] Angular Accel $\alpha$ [rad/s²] $a_t = r \alpha$ $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta$
Centripetal Accel $a_c$ Radial Accel $a_r$ $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r$ RPM Conversion: $\omega = \frac{\text{RPM} \cdot 2\pi}{60}$

⚖️ MODULE 2: NEWTONIAN DYNAMICS, INCLINES & ELEVATORS

🔴 ESSENTIAL

Newton's Laws of Motion

1st Law (Inertia): Body stays at rest or constant $v$ unless $\Sigma F \neq 0$.

2nd Law (Acceleration): $\Sigma \vec{F} = m \vec{a}$.

3rd Law (Action-Reaction): $\vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA}$ (equal magnitude, opposite direction, act on DIFFERENT bodies).

Inclined Plane Mechanics

• Parallel Force (Downslope): $F_\parallel = mg\sin\theta$

• Perpendicular Force (Normal): $N = mg\cos\theta$

• Friction Force: $f = \mu N = \mu mg\cos\theta$

• Acceleration down rough plane: $a = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k \cos\theta)$

• Angle of Repose (just starts sliding): $\mu_s = \tan\theta$

Elevator & Wall Friction Special Cases

Elevator Apparent Weight ($N$):

Accelerating UP ($+a$): $N = m(g + a) > W$

Accelerating DOWN ($-a$): $N = m(g - a) < W$

Cable Snapped (Free Fall): $N = 0$ (Weightless)

Vertical Wall Friction: $N = P \implies f_s = \mu P = mg \implies P_{min} = \frac{mg}{\mu_s}$.

⚡ MODULE 3: WORK, MECHANICAL ENERGY & POWER

🟠 HIGH-YIELD

Work & Mechanical Energy Principles

Work Definition: $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = F d \cos\theta = \int F \, dx$ [Joules = N·m]

Work-Energy Theorem: $W_{net} = \Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}m v_i^2$

Gravitational PE: $PE_g = m g h$

Elastic Spring PE: $PE_s = \frac{1}{2} k x^2$

Hanging Chain Work: $W = \frac{MgL}{18}$ (for $1/3$ length hanging over edge)

Power & Constant Engine Power Relations

Power Definition: $P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$ [Watts = J/s; $1\text{ hp} = 746\text{ W}$]

For car accelerating from rest under CONSTANT POWER $P$:

• $P = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}m v^2\right) \implies \frac{1}{2}m v^2 = P t$

• Velocity dependence: $v = \sqrt{\frac{2 P t}{m}} \propto t^{1/2}$

• Position dependence: $x = \int v \, dt \propto t^{3/2}$

💥 MODULE 4: IMPULSE, MOMENTUM & COLLISION MATRIX

🟠 HIGH-YIELD

Impulse & Momentum Theorem

• Linear Momentum: $\vec{p} = m \vec{v}$ [kg·m/s]

• Momentum vs KE: $KE = \frac{p^2}{2m} \implies p = \sqrt{2m KE}$

• $+20\%\text{ Momentum } p \implies +44\%\text{ Kinetic Energy } KE$

• Impulse: $\vec{J} = \int \vec{F} dt = \vec{F}_{avg} \Delta t = \Delta \vec{p}$

• Crumple Zone: $\uparrow \Delta t \implies \downarrow F_{avg}$ for same $\Delta p$.

Collision Classification

Elastic ($e = 1$): Momentum conserved, Kinetic Energy CONSERVED.

Inelastic ($0 < e < 1$): Momentum conserved, KE LOST to heat/deformation.

Completely Inelastic ($e = 0$): Objects stick together; maximum possible KE loss.

Coefficient of Restitution ($e$)

$e = \frac{v_{2f} - v_{1f}}{v_{1i} - v_{2i}} = \sqrt{\frac{h_{bounce}}{h_{drop}}}$

KE Loss Formula:

$\Delta KE_{loss} = \frac{1}{2} \mu (v_1 - v_2)^2 (1 - e^2)$

where reduced mass $\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$.

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE

Standard Moments of Inertia ($I = c \cdot m R^2$) Reference Table

Torque, Work & Power in Rotation

• Torque: $\tau = r F \sin\theta = I \alpha$ [N·m]

• Rotational Kinetic Energy: $KE_{rot} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$ [J]

• Total Rolling KE: $KE_{total} = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 + \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 (1 + c)$

• Rotational Work & Power: $W = \tau \theta, \quad P = \tau \omega$

Conservation of Angular Momentum ($L$)

• Angular Momentum: $L = I \omega$ [kg·m²/s or J·s]

• If no external torque ($\tau_{ext} = 0$) $\implies I_1 \omega_1 = I_2 \omega_2 = \text{const}$.

• Flywheel heated $\to R\uparrow \to I\uparrow \to \omega\downarrow$

• Skater pulls arms in $\to I\downarrow \to \omega\uparrow \to KE_{rot}\uparrow$ (work done by muscles!)

🌊 MODULE 6: SIMPLE HARMONIC MOTION & WAVE MECHANICS

🟠 HIGH-YIELD

Mass-Spring Oscillator

• Force: $F = -k x = m a \implies a = -\omega^2 x$

• Angular Frequency: $\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$

• Period & Frequency: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}, \quad f = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$

Spring Combinations:

Parallel: $k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2 = 2k \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}$

Series: $\frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2} = \frac{2}{k} \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{2m}}$

SHM Energy Partitioning

• Total Energy: $E = KE + PE = \frac{1}{2} k A^2$

• Position Equation: $x(t) = A \cos(\omega t)$

• Velocity Equation: $v(t) = -A \omega \sin(\omega t) \implies v_{max} = A \omega$

When $KE = 3 PE \implies E = 4 PE \implies \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = 4\left(\frac{1}{2}kx^2\right) \implies x = \pm \frac{A}{2}$

Simple Pendulum & Wave Speed

• Simple Pendulum: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$ (Independent of mass $m$!)

• Harmonic Wave: $y(x,t) = A \sin(k x - \omega t)$

• Wave Speed: $v = \frac{\omega}{k} = \lambda f$

• Speed on Stretched String: $v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$ ($\uparrow \text{Tension } T \implies \uparrow v$; $\uparrow \text{Linear Density } \mu \implies \downarrow v$)

📢 MODULE 7: ACOUSTICS, DECIBEL SCALE & DOPPLER EFFECT

🔴 ESSENTIAL

Sound Intensity & Decibel Level ($\beta$)

• Inverse Square Law: $I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2} \implies \frac{I_2}{I_1} = \left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2$

• Sound Intensity Level: $\beta = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)$ [dB], where $I_0 = 10^{-12}\text{ W/m}^2$

• Doubling Intensity ($2I$) increases sound level by $+3\text{ dB}$. $10\times$ Intensity increases level by $+10\text{ dB}$.

Doppler Effect General Formula

$$f_o = f_s \left( \frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s} \right)$$
  • • Observer moving TOWARD source: use $+v_o$ in numerator.
  • • Observer moving AWAY from source: use $-v_o$ in numerator.
  • • Source moving TOWARD observer: use $-v_s$ in denominator (higher pitch $f_o > f_s$).
  • • Source moving AWAY from observer: use $+v_s$ in denominator (lower pitch $f_o < f_s$).

Organ Pipe Resonance Rules

Open Pipe (Both Ends Open) $\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}, \quad f_n = n \left(\frac{v}{2L}\right) = n f_1 \quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$
Contains ALL integer harmonics (fundamental, 2nd, 3rd...)
Closed Pipe (One End Closed) $\lambda_n = \frac{4L}{n}, \quad f_n = n \left(\frac{v}{4L}\right) = n f_1 \quad (n = 1, 3, 5, \dots)$
Contains ONLY ODD harmonics (1st, 3rd, 5th...)

🔍 MODULE 8: GEOMETRIC OPTICS, MIRRORS & LENSMAKER'S FORMULA

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE

Refraction & Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

• Snell's Law: $n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2$ (where $n = c/v$)

• Critical Angle: $\sin\theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$

TIR Requirements: 1. $n_1 > n_2$ (Dense $\to$ Rare medium) AND 2. Incident angle $\theta_i > \theta_c$.

Thin Lens & Mirror Equations

• Mirror / Thin Lens Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$

• Magnification: $m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o} = \frac{h_i}{h_o}$

• Spherical Mirror Focal Length: $f = \pm \frac{R}{2}$

• Lensmaker's Equation: $\frac{1}{f} = (n - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$

Image Distance ($d_i$) Real Image (In front of mirror / Behind lens) Virtual Image (Behind mirror / In front of lens)

🌊 MODULE 9: FLUID MECHANICS & THERMAL EXPANSION

🟡 IMPORTANT

Hydrostatics & Pressure

• Hydrostatic Pressure: $P = P_0 + \rho g h$

• Gauge Pressure: $P_{gauge} = \rho g h$

• Archimedes' Principle (Buoyancy):

$F_B = \rho_{fluid} V_{submerged} g = W_{displaced}$

Fluid Dynamics

• Continuity Equation (Incompressible):

$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 \implies v_2 = v_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2$

• Bernoulli's Equation:

$P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{const}$

Thermal Expansion

• Linear Expansion: $\Delta L = \alpha L_0 \Delta T$

• Volume Expansion: $\Delta V = \beta V_0 \Delta T = 3\alpha V_0 \Delta T$

• Rotating flywheel expands $\to R\uparrow \to I\uparrow \to \omega\downarrow$

KINEMATICS 🔴 MUST MEMORIZE

PROJECTILE MAXIMUM HEIGHT EQUALS RANGE ($H = R$)

$$\tan\theta = 4 \implies \theta = \arctan(4) \approx 75.96^\circ$$

DERIVATION: $H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$, $R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} = \frac{2 v_0^2 \sin\theta \cos\theta}{g}$. Set $H=R \implies \frac{\sin\theta}{2} = 2\cos\theta \implies \tan\theta = 4$.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: Do NOT confuse with maximum range angle which is $\theta = 45^\circ$!

ENERGY & DYNAMICS 🟠 HIGH-YIELD

WORK TO PULL HANGING CHAIN ONTO TABLE

$$W = \Delta PE = m_{hang} g h_{cm} = \left(\frac{M}{3}\right) g \left(\frac{L}{6}\right) = \frac{M g L}{18}$$

VARIABLES: $M$ = Total Mass [kg], $L$ = Total Length [m], $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$.

ROTATION 🔴 MUST MEMORIZE

MOTOR TORQUE & ANGULAR ACCELERATION

$$\tau = I \alpha, \quad I_{disk} = \frac{1}{2} m R^2, \quad \alpha = \frac{\omega}{t} = \frac{2\pi f}{t}$$

VARIABLES: $\tau$ [N·m], $I$ [kg·m²], $\alpha$ [rad/s²], $f$ [rev/s or rpm/60].

EXAMPLE: $50\text{ kg}$ disk, $R=0.4\text{ m}$, $1200\text{ rpm}$ in $8\text{ s} \implies I=4\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2, \alpha=5\pi\text{ rad/s}^2, \tau=20\pi \approx 62.83\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: Convert rpm to rad/s first ($\omega = \frac{\text{rpm} \cdot 2\pi}{60}$).

OPTICS 🔴 MUST MEMORIZE

LENSMAKER'S EQUATION (THIN LENS)

$$\frac{1}{f} = (n - 1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right)$$

SIGN CONVENTION: Convex surface facing light $\implies R > 0$. Concave surface facing light $\implies R < 0$.

EXAMPLE: Biconvex $n=1.5, R_1 = +10\text{ cm}, R_2 = -15\text{ cm} \implies \frac{1}{f} = 0.5 \left(\frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{15}\right) = \frac{1}{12} \implies f = +12\text{ cm}$.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: $R_2$ for a biconvex lens is NEGATIVE in standard Cartesian convention!

ACOUSTICS 🟠 HIGH-YIELD

DOPPLER EFFECT (MOVING SOURCE)

$$f_o = f_s \left( \frac{v}{v \pm v_s} \right) \quad \text{(+ when moving AWAY, - when TOWARD)}$$

EXAMPLE: $1000\text{ Hz}$ siren moving away at $34\text{ m/s}$, $v=340\text{ m/s} \implies f_o = 1000 \left(\frac{340}{340 + 34}\right) = 909.09\text{ Hz}$.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: Source moving AWAY increases denominator, lowering observed frequency!

SHM 🟠 HIGH-YIELD

PARALLEL SPRINGS FREQUENCY & SHM ENERGY

$$k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2 = 2k \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}; \quad \text{When } KE = 3PE \implies x = \pm \frac{A}{2}$$

DERIVATION: Total $E = KE + PE = 4PE \implies \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = 4(\frac{1}{2}kx^2) \implies x^2 = A^2/4 \implies x = \pm A/2$.

DON'T CONFUSE THESE — HIGH-RISK BOARD EXAM TRAPS

⚠️ Side-by-Side Critical Comparisons

1. ROLLING VS SLIDING (WHEELS AT SAME SPEED)

Wheel A: Rolling without slipping

Has BOTH translational and rotational kinetic energy.

$KE_{total} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$

⚡ MORE Kinetic Energy!

Wheel B: Sliding on ice

Has ONLY translational kinetic energy (no rotation).

$KE_{total} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$

LESS Kinetic Energy

2. HOLLOW SPHERE VS SOLID CYLINDER ON INCLINE

Solid Cylinder ($I = \frac{1}{2}mr^2$)

Smaller shape factor $c = 0.5$. Less rotational inertia resist.

$a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + 1/2} = \frac{2}{3}g\sin\theta$

🏆 WINS RACE (Reaches bottom first)

Hollow Sphere ($I = \frac{2}{3}mr^2$)

Larger shape factor $c = 0.67$. Mass concentrated at edge.

$a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + 2/3} = \frac{3}{5}g\sin\theta$

Slower acceleration

3. BOUNCING RUBBER BALL VS STICKING CLAY BALL

Rubber Ball (Bounces back)

Velocity changes from $+v$ to $-v$.

$\Delta p = m(-v) - mv = -2mv$

⚡ Exerts $2\times$ GREATER Impulse on wall!

Clay Ball (Sticks to wall)

Velocity changes from $+v$ to $0$.

$\Delta p = 0 - mv = -mv$

Exerts half the impulse

4. HORIZONTAL INCLINE SLIDING VS CONSTANT VELOCITY

Impending Motion (Static)

Angle where block JUST begins to slide.

$\mu_s = \tan\theta_{repose}$

Constant Velocity Motion (Kinetic)

Sliding down at constant speed ($a=0$).

$\mu_k = \tan\theta$

🌳 DECISION TREE: WHICH INCLINE / FRICTION EQUATION TO USE?

1. Is block pressed against vertical wall?
└─ YES $\to N = P \implies f_s = \mu P = mg \implies P_{min} = \frac{mg}{\mu}$
2. Is block on inclined plane $\theta$?
├─ Normal force $N = mg\cos\theta$
├─ Downslope force $F_\parallel = mg\sin\theta$
└─ Just sliding on own weight $\to \mu = \tan\theta$
3. Is block sliding at CONSTANT VELOCITY down incline?
└─ YES $\to F_{net} = 0 \implies \mu_k = \tan\theta$

🌳 DECISION TREE: ROTATIONAL MOTION PROBLEMS

1. Is external torque applied? ($\tau_{ext} \neq 0$)
└─ YES $\to$ Use $\tau = I\alpha$, where $\alpha = \Delta\omega / t$
2. No external torque? ($\tau_{ext} = 0$)
└─ YES $\to$ Conserve Angular Momentum: $I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2$
3. Is object rolling down incline without slipping?
└─ YES $\to a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + c}$ ($c=1/2$ disk, $c=2/3$ hollow sphere)
