Strict Variable Definitions & Limiting Conditions
🔴 MUST MEMORIZE EST-AM-01

AM (DSB-FC) Total Power & Sideband Split

$$P_t = P_c \left(1 + \frac{m^2}{2}\right)$$
$$P_{SB} = P_c \left(\frac{m^2}{2}\right), \quad P_{LSB} = P_{USB} = P_c \left(\frac{m^2}{4}\right)$$

Variables: $P_t$ = Total power [W], $P_c$ = Carrier power [W], $m$ = Modulation index ($0 \le m \le 1$).

Proportionality: Sideband power scales quadratically: $\frac{P_{SB2}}{P_{SB1}} = \left(\frac{m_2}{m_1}\right)^2$.

USE WHEN: Calculating transmitter power changes when modulating audio increases/decreases.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: For a single sideband (SSB), the denominator is 4, not 2! When switching DSB-FC to SSB with suppressed carrier, carrier drops to 0 W and only 1 sideband radiates ($P_{SSB} = \frac{m^2 P_c}{4}$).
🔴 MUST MEMORIZE EST-FM-02

Carson's Rule & Modulation Index

$$BW = 2(\Delta f + f_{m(\max)}) = 2f_{m(\max)}(\beta + 1)$$
$$\beta = \frac{\Delta f}{f_m}, \quad DR = \frac{\Delta f_{\max}}{f_{m(\max)}}$$

Variables: $\Delta f$ = Frequency deviation [Hz], $f_m$ = Audio modulating frequency [Hz], $\beta$ = Modulation index [dimensionless / rad].

USE WHEN: Broadcast channel bandwidth allocation, frequency multipliers, Deviation Ratio (DR).

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: Passing an FM wave through a frequency doubler multiplies both $f_c$ and $\Delta f$ by 2, causing $\beta$ to double to $2m$. The audio pitch $f_m$ never changes!
🔴 MUST MEMORIZE EST-RX-03

Superhet Frequencies & Image Rejection (IRR)

$$f_{LO} = f_s + f_{IF}, \quad f_{si} = f_s + 2f_{IF}$$
$$IRR = \sqrt{1 + Q^2 \rho^2}, \quad \rho = \frac{f_{si}}{f_s} - \frac{f_s}{f_{si}}$$ $$IRR_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(IRR)$$

Variables: $f_s$ = Desired station, $f_{IF}$ = Standard IF (AM = $455\text{ kHz}$, FM = $10.7\text{ MHz}$), $Q$ = Quality factor of RF pre-selector tank.

USE WHEN: Computing local oscillator tuning frequency and front-end image attenuation.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: Image rejection occurs only in the RF pre-selector before the mixer! The IF amp provides adjacent-channel selectivity, not image rejection.
🔴 MUST MEMORIZE EST-ANT-04

Antenna Efficiency & Link Budget (EIRP)

$$\eta = \frac{R_{\text{rad}}}{R_{\text{rad}} + R_{\text{loss}}} = \frac{R_{\text{rad}}}{R_{\text{in}}}$$
$$G = \eta \cdot D \implies G_{\text{dBi}} = 10\log_{10}(G)$$ $$EIRP_{\text{dBm}} = P_{t(\text{dBm})} - L_{\text{cable(dB)}} + G_{\text{ant(dBi)}}$$ $$EIRP_{\text{dBW}} = EIRP_{\text{dBm}} - 30$$

Variables: $R_{\text{rad}}$ = Radiation resistance [$\Omega$], $R_{\text{loss}}$ = Loss resistance [$\Omega$], $D$ = Directivity, $EIRP$ = Effective Isotropic Radiated Power.

USE WHEN: Transmitter radiated power evaluations and transmitter-antenna match.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: $0\text{ dBW} = +30\text{ dBm}$. Always subtract 30 when converting from $\text{dBm}$ to $\text{dBW}$. Directivity $D$ equals gain $G$ only if efficiency is 100% ($\eta = 1.0$).
🟠 HIGH-YIELD EST-ANT-05

Parabolic Reflector Beamwidth & Gain

$$\theta_{\text{3dB}} \approx \frac{70 \lambda}{D} \quad [\text{degrees}]$$
$$G = \eta \left(\frac{\pi D}{\lambda}\right)^2, \quad \lambda = \frac{c}{f}$$

Variables: $\theta_{\text{3dB}}$ = Half-Power Beamwidth [deg], $D$ = Reflector diameter [m], $\lambda$ = Wavelength [m], $\eta$ = Aperture efficiency ($0.55 - 0.65$ typical).

USE WHEN: Microwave backhaul links, satellite ground stations, radar dish sizing.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: $D$ and $\lambda$ must be in identical units (meters). Speed of light $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$. If diameter is in centimeters, convert to meters first!
🟠 HIGH-YIELD EST-RX-06

Dipole Cut Length & Receiver Dynamic Range

$$L_{\text{actual}} = k \cdot \frac{\lambda}{2} = (1 - \text{end effect}) \cdot \frac{c}{2f}$$
$$DR_{\text{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_{\max}}{P_{\min}}\right) \implies P_{\max} = P_{\min} \cdot 10^{\frac{DR}{10}}$$

Variables: $k = 0.95$ for standard 5% end effect / velocity shortening factor; $P_{\min}$ = sensitivity threshold; $P_{\max}$ = upper overload limit.

USE WHEN: Practical antenna fabrication cut-lengths and receiver overload threshold.

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: A half-wave dipole at resonance has $Z_{\text{in}} \approx 72\ \Omega$. Operating slightly above resonance makes it inductive ($+jX$); operating slightly below makes it capacitive ($-jX$).

Analog Modulation Architectures (AM, SSB, FM, PM)

Analog-to-Analog Conversion
Fundamental Classification [NOTES: Q1]

AM, FM, and PM are Strictly "Analog to Analog" Conversions

Continuous-time analog audio baseband ($0\text{--}15\text{ kHz}$) directly alters the amplitude, frequency, or phase of a continuous sinusoidal RF carrier wave ($535\text{ kHz to }108\text{ MHz}$).

Voice/Audio $m(t)$ ➔ Modulator ➔ Analog RF $s(t)$

Given $f_c = 5.000\text{ MHz}$, audio $300\text{ Hz to } 3,000\text{ Hz}$:

USB: $(5.000\text{ MHz} + 300\text{ Hz}) \to (5.000\text{ MHz} + 3000\text{ Hz}) = \mathbf{5,000,300\text{ to }5,003,000\text{ Hz}}$

Frequency Modulation (FM) vs Phase Modulation (PM)

Q8 - Q21
Frequency Modulation (FM)
$$\Delta f = k_f V_m$$

Peak deviation $\Delta f$ depends strictly on modulating amplitude $V_m$.

$$\beta = \frac{\Delta f}{f_m}$$

If audio pitch $f_m$ increases, $\beta$ decreases inversely, but $\Delta f$ stays constant!

Phase Modulation (PM)
$$\Delta f = k_p V_m \cdot f_m$$

Peak deviation $\Delta f$ is proportional to amplitude $V_m$ AND audio frequency $f_m$.

$$m_p = \Delta \theta = k_p V_m$$

Modulation index $m_p$ is pure phase deviation in radians, independent of $f_m$.

🔴 CRITICAL BOARD EXAM EXPERIMENT [Q19]:
Audio frequency $f_m$ increased from $2\text{ kHz} \to 4\text{ kHz}$ at constant audio voltage $V_m$ ($\Delta f_{\text{initial}} = 10\text{ kHz}$):
• FM new $\Delta f = \mathbf{10\text{ kHz}}$ (Constant) • PM new $\Delta f = \mathbf{20\text{ kHz}}$ (Doubled!)

Superheterodyne Receiver Architectures & Diagnostics

Image Frequency & Selectivity Engines
Signal Processing Stage & Function Matrix Standard High-Side: $f_{LO} = f_s + f_{IF}$
STAGE 1 Q27

RF Amplifier / Pre-selector

  • • Sets receiver Sensitivity (low NF).
  • • STRICTLY REJECTS Image Freq ($f_{si}$) before mixing!
  • • Tuned synchronously with Local Oscillator (ganged tuning).
STAGE 2 Q29, Q30

Mixer + Local Oscillator

  • • Mixes incoming $f_s$ with $f_{LO}$ to downconvert to fixed $f_{IF}$.
  • • Standard High-Side AM: $f_{LO} = f_s + 455\text{ kHz}$.
  • • Standard FM: $f_{IF} = 10.7\text{ MHz}$.
  • • Non-linear device generates sum & difference components.
STAGE 3 Q24, Q26

IF Amplifier (Tuned Filter)

  • • Provides bulk of receiver gain ($>60\text{ dB}$).
  • • Selectivity: Sharp filter rejects adjacent channels.
  • • Operating frequency is permanently fixed regardless of tuning.
  • • Cannot reject image because image is already converted to $f_{IF}$!
STAGE 4 Q25, Q28

Demodulator & Audio Amp

  • • AM: Envelope detector extracts carrier amplitude variations.
  • • FM: Discriminator / ratio detector extracts frequency changes.
  • • Capture Effect: FM receiver suppresses weaker signal on the same frequency.

Antennas, Directivity & Wave Propagation

Field Orientation & Geometry Rules
POLARIZATION ENGINE Q45 - Q49

Electric Field Vector ($\vec{E}$) Rules

• Definition [Q45]: Polarization is defined by the spatial orientation of the Electric Field Vector ($\vec{E}$) relative to Earth.

• Smooth Ground Reflection [Q46]: Horizontally polarized wave reflecting off smooth flat ground remains horizontally polarized (incurs $180^\circ$ phase shift at grazing angles).

• Over Bodies of Water [Q47]: Horizontal polarization is significantly superior for low antenna heights (vertical suffers extreme ground wave tilt and dielectric absorption).

• High Above Ground [Q48]: Horizontally polarized antennas provide maximum signal strength reaching the surface.

• Cross-Polarization [Q49]: Horizontal transmitter sending to vertical receiver results in complete signal cancellation (0 Watts / theoretical $\infty\text{ dB}$ loss).

RESONANCE & IMPEDANCE Q34 - Q41

Physical Length & Frequency Behavior

• Resonant Determinant [Q36, Q38]: Dictated strictly by wavelength ($\lambda = c/f$) and physical geometry. Feedline impedance matching does not alter resonant frequency!

• Alter Radiation Pattern [Q35]: Most effective method is changing the physical geometry of the antenna.

• Improper Impedance Match [Q34]: Severe mismatch causes standing waves and reduces overall power transfer efficiency.

• Off-Resonance Impedance [Q39, Q40]: Above $f_0$: Physically too long ➔ Inductive (+jX) Below $f_0$: Physically too short ➔ Capacitive (-jX)

• Conductor Thickness [Q41]: Increasing wire diameter increases antenna bandwidth (decreases effective $Q$).

TYPES & DIRECTIVITY Q33, Q37, Q42 - Q44

Antenna Types & Directivity Laws

• Gain Definition [Q33]: Ratio of power radiated in a given direction compared to an isotropic radiator.

• Directivity vs Beamwidth [Q37, Q43]: Inverse relationship: Higher directivity = Narrower beamwidth.

• Isotropic Radiator [Q42]: Theoretical lossless point source whose radiation pattern is a perfect 3D sphere ($G = 0\text{ dBi}$).

• Yagi-Uda Arrays [Q44]: Adding more director elements decreases beamwidth and increases directivity.

• Front-to-Back Ratio (FBR) [Q55]: $$\text{FBR} = G_{\text{front}} - G_{\text{back}} = 14\text{ dBi} - (-8\text{ dBi}) = \mathbf{22\text{ dB}}$$

Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) & Digital Communications

Discretization & Regeneration Laws

The Canonical 3-Step PCM Conversion Pipeline

Q58
STEP 1: SAMPLING (PAM) [Q58 Target]

Converts continuous-time, continuous-amplitude signal into a discrete-time, continuous-amplitude pulse stream. Governed by Nyquist criterion ($f_s \ge 2 f_{\max}$).

STEP 2: QUANTIZATION

Converts continuous amplitude to discrete amplitude levels. Introduces irreversible Quantization Error / Noise ($Q_e$).

STEP 3: ENCODING

Assigns a unique binary codeword ($n$ bits) to each quantized voltage level ($L = 2^n$).

Repeaters vs Amplifiers & Noise Immunity

Q56, Q57
Analog Amplifiers (Cascaded Accumulation):

Boost signal voltage, but simultaneously amplify incoming line noise and add their own thermal noise. Noise accumulates monotonically with distance, severely degrading SNR.

Digital Regenerative Repeaters [Q57 Advantage]:

Perform threshold detection, re-timing, and generate completely brand-new clean pulses. Channel noise is eliminated at every repeater!

Noise Immunity Principle [Q56]: Digital signals are more immune to noise because receivers distinguish only between discrete voltage states (0 or 1), rather than infinitesimally small analog variations.

High-Probability Multiple-Choice Traps
SELECTIVITY vs SENSITIVITY
Selectivity: Ability to separate closely spaced stations and reject adjacent channels. Governed strictly by the IF Amplifier filter shape.
Sensitivity: Ability to detect extremely weak signals (e.g., remote province). Governed by RF stage gain and noise figure.
Trap: Remoteness requires sensitivity; crowded dials require selectivity!
RF PRE-SELECTOR vs IF AMPLIFIER
RF Pre-selector: Must reject the Image Frequency ($f_s + 2f_{IF}$) prior to the mixer.
IF Amplifier: Rejects adjacent channels. It cannot reject image frequencies because the mixer already converted both to $f_{IF}$!
Trap: Image rejection is 100% finished before the mixer.
RESONANCE vs IMPEDANCE MATCHING
Resonant Frequency: Dictated purely by physical antenna length $L \approx \lambda/2$.
Impedance Matching: Matching cable to antenna optimizes power transfer, but does NOT alter the natural resonant frequency of the antenna!
Trap: Tuning an antenna tuner does not change wire physical resonance.
FM DEVIATION vs PM DEVIATION
In FM: Deviation $\Delta f$ depends ONLY on modulating audio amplitude $V_m$. Pitch change has zero effect on $\Delta f$.
In PM: Deviation $\Delta f$ is proportional to audio amplitude $V_m$ AND audio frequency $f_m$. Doubling audio pitch doubles deviation!
Trap: Modulation index in PM ($m_p = \Delta \theta$) is constant with frequency.
FM TRANSMITTED POWER vs AM TRANSMITTED POWER
FM Power: Remains strictly constant as modulation increases. Carrier gives energy up to sidebands ($P_t = V_c^2 / 2R$).
AM Power: Increases quadratically as modulation increases ($P_t = P_c(1 + m^2/2)$).
Trap: AM ammeter reading rises with voice; FM ammeter remains rock solid!
HIGH IF ADVANTAGE vs LOW IF ADVANTAGE
High IF: Pushes image frequency further away ($2f_{IF}$), dramatically improving Image Rejection.
Low IF: Easier to build high-gain, narrow bandpass filters, improving Adjacent Channel Selectivity.
Trap: Dual-conversion receivers use High IF first (image), then Low IF (selectivity)!
