1. TRANSDUCERS & SENSORS (INDUSTRIAL & OPTICAL)

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE

Active Transducers (Self-Generating)

No External DC/AC

Converts physical energy directly into an electrical voltage or current without any external excitation power source.

Examples to recall:
  • Thermocouple (Seebeck thermoelectric mV)
  • Piezoelectric crystal (Force $\to$ surface charge)
  • Photovoltaic (Solar) cell (Photons $\to$ DC power)
  • Dynamic microphone (Acoustic wave $\to$ induced emf)

Passive Transducers (Externally Powered)

Requires Excitation

Requires an external DC or AC excitation voltage/current. Modulates parameters ($R, L, C$) in response to physical changes.

Examples to recall:
  • RTD (Pt100) ($\Delta R$ metal, highly linear)
  • Thermistor (NTC/PTC metal-oxide semiconductor)
  • LVDT (Mutual AC inductance for displacement)
  • Strain Gauge & LDR (Photoconductive CdS cell)
Sensor Physical Principle Output Linearity Temp. Coeff. Key Exam Traps & Characteristics

Optical Transducers

Photodiode: Operates on internal Photoelectric Effect. Max switching speed & fast response achieved strictly under REVERSE BIAS (minimizes junction capacitance $C_j$, expands depletion zone).

Laser Diode vs. LED: Laser produces coherent, monochromatic light. LED produces incoherent, broad-spectrum light.

LDR (Photoconductive cell): Cadmium Sulfide (CdS). Incident light $\uparrow \implies R \downarrow$ non-linearly.

Optocoupler: LED + Phototransistor. Delivers Galvanic Isolation between high-voltage field lines and MCU logic.

Displacement & Force

LVDT: Electromechanical transformer. Measures linear position via differential AC voltage coupling. Zero friction, infinite mechanical life, exceptional resolution.

Strain Gauge: Metal foil pattern. Changes resistance with mechanical strain: $\Delta R / R = GF \cdot \epsilon$.

Piezoelectric Crystal: Quartz / PZT ceramic generates voltage under mechanical stress. TRAP: Measures DYNAMIC stress only; cannot measure static loads because charge leaks through amplifier impedance.

Proximity & Acoustic

Inductive Proximity: Detects FERROUS/METALLIC targets only via eddy current losses.

Capacitive Proximity: Detects METALLIC & NON-METALLIC materials (liquids, plastics, bulk solids) via dielectric constant changes.

Microphones: Acoustic pressure waves $\to$ electrical AC signals (dynamic, condenser, MEMS).

Cabling Shielding & Spacing: PEC dictates instrument signaling lines must maintain physical separation from $60\text{ Hz}$ AC power lines to stop Crosstalk & EMI.

2. INDUSTRIAL AUTOMATION — PLCs & SCADA

🔴 HIGH-YIELD

PLC Hardware Architecture

  • CPU Module: Executes control ladder logic, controls memory, diagnostics, and communication channels.
  • Input Modules: Condition external field inputs ($24\text{ V DC}, 220\text{ V AC}, 4\text{--}20\text{ mA}$) with internal optical isolation.
  • Output Modules: Drive field actuators (relays, triacs, transistors) from internal output image tables.
  • Watchdog Timer (WDT): Hardware/software fail-safe mechanism dedicated to detecting CPU freezing or program loop execution failure. Forces outputs de-energized if logic scan times out.
  • PLC vs. MCU: PLCs provide ruggedized casing, optical isolation, high noise immunity, and modular field I/O expansion.

The 4-Phase PLC Scan Cycle

1
Input Scan: Read physical inputs into Input Image Table.
2
Logic Scan: Execute ladder logic rungs top-to-bottom.
3
Diagnostics / Comms: Self-checks, reset WDT, comms.
4
Output Scan: Write Output Image Table to physical field contacts.
EXAM TRAP: Field outputs are never updated rung-by-rung during program execution! They update ONLY during the Output Scan phase.

SCADA & IEC 61131-3 Languages

Ladder Diagram (LD): Most widely used in industry; mirrors electromechanical relay contacts and coils.

Structured Text (ST): High-level Pascal-like text language for complex mathematics and array algorithms.

Function Block Diagram (FBD): Connects functional blocks (PID, timers) like electronic circuit diagrams.

Sequential Function Chart (SFC): State machine flowchart for batch processes.

SCADA Field Layer: RTU (Remote Terminal Unit) interfaces directly with field sensors/actuators to execute local control and stream telemetry to the MTU (Master Terminal Unit).

3. SMART BUILDING SYSTEMS (BMS)

HVAC & SECURITY
Primary Goal of HVAC Integration:

To automate and optimize energy consumption while sustaining indoor environmental quality, temperature, and fresh-air volume.

Peak Demand Reduction Techniques:
  • Load Shedding: Turn off non-essential loads (decorative lighting, secondary pumps).
  • Staggered Equipment Startup: Start heavy chillers/AHUs sequentially to avoid aggregate inductive inrush current spikes.
  • Chilled Water Reset: Elevate chilled water temperature setpoints during partial building load periods.
  • FORBIDDEN: Never disable life-safety fire detection loops to save energy!

Fail-Safe Emergency Door Locks: In smart buildings, access control doors must be wired FAIL-SAFE (automatically unlock on loss of power or upon receiving a confirmed fire alarm signal) to permit unhindered emergency egress.

HVAC Response to Fire Alarm: FACP commands BMS to shut down supply fans and close fire/smoke dampers (prevents feeding oxygen and spreading smoke), while initiating stairwell pressurization fans.

CCTV H.265 (HEVC) Compression: Delivers up to 50% bandwidth and storage reduction over older H.264 (AVC) at equivalent resolution.

Sub-metering: Measures power/water per floor or tenant to enable accurate tenant billing and leak identification.

5. DIGITAL COMMUNICATIONS — LINE CODING SCHEMES

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE

Line Coding Definition & Bit Mapping

The process of converting digital data (binary bit stream) into discrete digital signals (voltage pulses) over a transmission link.

Average Baud: $S_{\text{avg}} = c \cdot N \cdot \frac{1}{r}$  |  ($c = 0.5$ typically)
Bit Stream: [ 1 ] [ 0 ] [ 1 ] [ 1 ] [ 0 ] [ 0 ] [ 1 ] Normalized Clock Cycles
Polar NRZ-L vs. NRZ-I
  • • NRZ-L: The voltage level directly corresponds to the bit value. Subject to baseline wander & DC bias.
  • • NRZ-I: Inverts signal at start of bit for Logic 1; keeps level for Logic 0. Immune to polarity inversion.
  • • Baud Rate: $S_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{2}N$.
Manchester & Differential
  • • Manchester: Mid-bit transition always ($0 = \text{Hi}\to\text{Lo}$, $1 = \text{Lo}\to\text{Hi}$). Zero DC bias, self-clocking.
  • • EXAM TRAP: Manchester requires 2× the bandwidth of NRZ ($S_{\text{avg}} = N$).
  • • Differential Manchester: Transition at start of bit $= 0$; no start transition $= 1$.
Bipolar AMI & 2B1Q
  • • Bipolar AMI: 3 levels ($+V, 0, -V$). Bit $0 = 0\text{ V}$. Bit $1 = \text{alternating } +V \text{ and } -V$. Zero DC component.
  • • 2B1Q Scheme: Encodes 2 bits into 1 quaternary element ($-3, -1, +1, +3\text{ V}$).
  • • Halves the bandwidth requirement ($r = 2$ bits/signal, $S_{\text{avg}} = N/4$).

6. FORMULA REFERENCE CARDS & EXAM CALCULATORS

🟠 HIGH-YIELD COMPUTATIONS

FORMULA CARD 1: PHOTODIODE RESPONSIVITY

Exam Q12
$$R = \frac{I_p}{P} = \frac{\eta \cdot q}{h \cdot \nu} = \frac{\eta \cdot q \cdot \lambda}{h \cdot c}$$
Photocurrent: $I_p = R \cdot P$

Variables: $R = \text{Responsivity [A/W]}$, $I_p = \text{Photocurrent [A]}$, $P = \text{Optical Power [W]}$, $\eta = \text{Quantum Efficiency}$, $\lambda = \text{Wavelength [m]}$.

Constants: $q = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$, $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.

Interactive Solver (Verify Exam Q12):
Ip = 17.00 µA

FORMULA CARD 2: ADC VOLTAGE RESOLUTION

Exam Q18
$$\text{Voltage Resolution (LSB)} = \frac{V_{\max} - V_{\min}}{2^n}$$
Dynamic Range: $DR \approx 2^n$

Variables: $\text{FSR} = V_{\max} - V_{\min} = \text{Full Scale Range [V]}$, $n = \text{ADC Resolution [bits]}$, $2^n = \text{Quantization Levels}$.

Trap Alert: Do not divide by $2^n - 1$ for standard industrial step sizing unless code transition spans are specifically requested.

Interactive Solver (Verify Exam Q18):
Res = 2.44 mV
