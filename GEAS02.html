1. Atomic Structure & Trends

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE
FORMULA: Neutron Number ($N$) [Q2]
$N = A - Z$
Variables: $A$ = Mass Number [amu], $Z$ = Atomic Number [protons]
USE WHEN: Finding neutron count in isotopes or nuclear reactions.
CONDITION: For neutral atom, Protons ($p^+$) = Electrons ($e^-$) = $Z$.
COMMON TRAP: Do not confuse Atomic Number $Z$ (protons) with Mass Number $A$ ($p^+ + n$).
Periodic Trends Notes (Li → Ne) [Q3]:

Moving left to right across Period 2: Atomic radius UNIFORMLY DECREASES because effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$) increases with added protons without adding new electron shells.

Period Boundaries [Q1]:

• Start of Periods: Alkali Metals (Group 1 - Highly reactive)
• End of Periods: Inert / Noble Gases (Group 18 - Full octet valence shell)

Chemical Bonding [Q15]:

• Nonmetal + Nonmetal $\longrightarrow$ Covalent Bond (Electron sharing)
• Metal + Nonmetal $\longrightarrow$ Ionic Bond (Electron transfer)

2. Mole Concept & Calculations

⚠️ COMMON TRAP
1 Mole Equivalencies (What is NOT 1 mol?):

• 22.414 L $N_2$ gas at STP = 1 mol
• $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ $O_2$ molecules = 1 mol
• 12 g Carbon atoms ($C=12$) = 1 mol
• 16 g Oxygen gas ($O_2$) = 0.5 mol! (Since $O_2 = 32\text{ g/mol}$). [Q4]

Step-by-Step Solution: Arsenic Acid [Q5]
Given: $1\text{ kg } H_3AsO_3$, $As=74.9\text{ amu}$
Step 1 (MW): $MW = 3(1.008) + 74.9 + 3(16.00) = \mathbf{125.92\text{ g/mol}}$
Step 2 (Moles): $n = \frac{1000\text{ g}}{125.92\text{ g/mol}} = \mathbf{7.94\text{ moles}}$
Step 3 (Molecules): $N = 7.94 \times 6.022\times 10^{23} = \mathbf{4.78 \times 10^{24}\text{ molecules}}$

3. Semiconductor Physics & Doping

🟠 HIGH-YIELD
Intrinsic Germanium + Phosphorus Doping [Q17]:

Phosphorus (Group V pentavalent) donates extra free electrons $\implies$ Creates n-type semiconductor; majority carriers: Electrons, minority: Holes.

Temperature vs Semiconductor Conductivity [Q18]:

Intrinsic Semiconductor conductivity increases exponentially with temperature because thermal energy excites valence electrons across the bandgap ($E_g$) into the conduction band.

Gallium Arsenide (GaAs) vs Silicon [Q19]:

GaAs features higher electron mobility and a direct energy bandgap $\implies$ superior speed for high-frequency RF and optoelectronic devices.

Section 02

1. Ideal Gas Density Equation

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE
FORMULA: Gas Mass Density ($\rho$) [Q6]
$\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{P \cdot MW}{R \cdot T}$
Variables: $P$ [atm], $MW$ [g/mol], $R = 0.0821\text{ L}\cdot\text{atm/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$, $T$ [K]
USE WHEN: Finding gas density at non-STP conditions.
RELATIONSHIP: Density $\rho \propto P$ and $\rho \propto 1/T$.
COMMON TRAP: Temperatures MUST be converted to Absolute Kelvin ($K = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15$).
⚠️ REAL GAS DEVIATION TRAP [Q7]:

• Real gases behave IDEALLY at: High T & Low P
• Real gases show GREATEST DEVIATION from ideal behavior at: LOW Temperatures and HIGH Pressures (intermolecular forces & gas volume become significant).

2. Graham's Law & RMS Velocity

🟠 HIGH-YIELD
FORMULA: Graham's Law of Effusion [Q10]
$\frac{\text{Rate}_1}{\text{Rate}_2} = \sqrt{\frac{MW_2}{MW_1}}$
Variables: $\text{Rate}$ [moles/s], $MW$ [g/mol]
USE WHEN: Comparing effusion or diffusion speeds of two gases.
TRAP: Rate ratio is INVERSE to square root of Molecular Weight!
FORMULA: Root-Mean-Square Speed ($v_{rms}$) [Q11]
$v_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{MW}} = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}} \implies v_{rms} \propto \sqrt{T}$
USE WHEN: Finding molecular speed of gas particles.
QUADRUPLE T RULE: Quadrupling absolute $T$ ($4T$) increases $v_{rms}$ by $\sqrt{4} = \mathbf{2.0\text{ times (DOUBLES)}}$.

3. Solved Gas Law Problems

🔵 COMPUTATIONAL
Dalton's Law: Gas Over Water [Q8] P_dry = P_total - P_H2O
$P_{total} = 755\text{ mmHg}, P_{H2O} = 23.8\text{ mmHg}$
$P_{dry O2} = 755 - 23.8 = \mathbf{731.2\text{ mmHg}}$
• Pressure Units: $1\text{ atm} = 760\text{ mmHg} = 760\text{ torr} = 101,325\text{ Pa} = 1.01325\text{ bar}$
Charle's Law & Mass Calculation [Q9]
$V_1 = 2.5\text{ L } H_2$ at STP ($273.15\text{ K}$), $T_2 = 25^\circ\text{C} = 298.15\text{ K}$
$V_2 = V_1 \left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) = 2.5 \left(\frac{298.15}{273.15}\right) = \mathbf{2.73\text{ L}}$
$\text{Mass } H_2 = \frac{2.5\text{ L}}{22.414\text{ L/mol}} \times 2.016\text{ g/mol} = \mathbf{0.22\text{ g}}$

1. Alpha Decay & Particle Physics

3. Solved Radioactive Decay

🔵 COMPUTATIONAL
Step-by-Step Decay Solution [Q37]

80% remains after 10 yrs ($N(10) = 0.80 N_0$). How much remains after 90 additional years ($t_{total} = 100\text{ yrs}$)?

General Form: $N(t) = N_0 \cdot (k)^{t / 10}$ where $k = 0.80$
At $t = 100\text{ yrs}: N(100) = N_0 \cdot (0.80)^{100/10} = N_0 \cdot (0.80)^{10}$
$(0.80)^{10} = 0.10737 \approx \mathbf{11\%\text{ remaining}}$

Section 04

1. First Law Thermodynamics

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE
FORMULA: First Law ($\Delta U$) [Q40, Q41, Q42]
$\Delta U = Q - W_{\text{by}} = Q + W_{\text{on}}$
ADIABATIC ($Q=0$): $\Delta U = W_{\text{on}}$. If $\Delta U = +20\text{ J}$, $20\text{ J}$ work done ON system. Expansion ($W_{\text{by}}>0$) $\implies$ Temp DECREASES.
ISOTHERMAL ($\Delta T=0 \implies \Delta U=0$): $W_{\text{by}} = Q$ (Work done equals heat absorbed).

2. Fourier & Stefan-Boltzmann

🟠 HIGH-YIELD
FORMULA: Fourier's Law Conduction [Q43, Q45]
$\frac{Q}{t} = \frac{k \cdot A \cdot \Delta T}{L}$
Variables: $k$ [W/(m·K)], $A$ [m²], $\Delta T$ [K or °C], $L$ [m]
THICKNESS RULE: Doubling wall thickness $L$ REDUCES heat transfer rate by HALF.
FORMULA: Stefan-Boltzmann Radiation [Q44, Q46]
$\frac{Q}{t} = \varepsilon \cdot \sigma \cdot A \cdot \left(T_s^4 - T_{env}^4\right)$
Constants: $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\text{ W/(m}^2\text{K}^4)$, Emissivity $\varepsilon \in [0, 1]$.
TRAP: $T_s$ and $T_{env}$ MUST be in Kelvin before taking 4th power!

3. Carnot Engine & COP

⚠️ COMMON TRAP
FORMULA: Carnot Efficiency ($\eta_{\text{max}}$) [Q47, Q48]
$\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{Q_H}$
2ND LAW LIMIT: No real heat engine can exceed Carnot efficiency!
HEAT PUMP REL: $COP(hp) = COP(ref) + 1$ [Q49]

Solved Board Exam Numerical Problems in Thermodynamics

Thermal Contact Equilibrium [Q39]
$C_B = 2 C_A$, $T_{A,i}=300\text{ K}, T_{B,i}=450\text{ K}$
$-Q_{lost} = +Q_{gained} \implies -C_B(T_f - T_{B,i}) = C_A(T_f - T_{A,i})$
$-2(T_f - 450) = (T_f - 300) \implies 3T_f = 1200$
$T_f = 400\text{ K}$
Firebrick Conduction [Q45]
$L=4\text{ in}, k=9\frac{\text{BTU}}{\text{ft}^2\cdot\text{hr}\cdot^\circ\text{F}/\text{in}}, A=2\text{ ft}^2$
$\Delta T = 300 - 90 = 210^\circ\text{F}, t = 24\text{ hrs}$
$Q = \frac{9 \times 2 \times 210}{4} \times 24$
$Q = 22,680\text{ BTU}$
Sphere Radiative Loss [Q46]
$r=0.05\text{ m } (A=4\pi r^2 = 0.031416\text{ m}^2)$
$\varepsilon=0.8, T_s=500\text{ K}, T_{env}=300\text{ K}$
$\frac{Q}{t} = (0.8)(5.67\times 10^{-8})(0.031416)(500^4 - 300^4)$
$\frac{Q}{t} = 77.5\text{ W}$
Inventor Claim Feasibility [Q48]
$T_H = 500^\circ\text{C} = 773\text{ K}, T_C = 20^\circ\text{C} = 293\text{ K}$
Claimed $\eta = \frac{500\text{ kJ}}{800\text{ kJ}} = \mathbf{62.5\%}$
$\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{293}{773} = \mathbf{62.08\%}$
IMPOSSIBLE! Claimed $>$ Carnot
