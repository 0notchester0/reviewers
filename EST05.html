Master Overview & Propagation Classification

The 3 foundational mechanisms governed by frequency, medium, and boundaries

HIGH-YIELD 🟠
VLF / LF / MF 30 kHz – 3 MHz

Ground Wave (Surface Wave)

Diffracts along the Earth's curvature guided by electrical conductivity. Uses vertical polarization to prevent horizontal short-circuiting against ground.

  • Best on seawater & high-conductivity plains [Q7]
  • Range drops sharply as freq increases [Q8]
  • Completely unaffected by ionosphere changes [Q9]
Key Application: AM Broadcast (e.g., 630 kHz [Q7]), Submarine LF, Maritime distress.
HF Band 3 MHz – 30 MHz

Skywave (Ionospheric)

Total internal refraction/reflection from ionized gases in upper atmosphere ($60\text{ km} - 400\text{ km}$). Enables transoceanic multi-thousand km paths.

  • Much longer range than ground waves [Q4]
  • Reflects at 15–21 MHz over 500–3000 km [Q2, Q3]
  • Creates a Skip Zone (dead zone) near Tx [Q11, Q12]
Key Application: International shortwave, transoceanic HF, emergency ham radio.
VHF / UHF / SHF > 30 MHz

Space Wave (Tropospheric / LOS)

Direct ray + ground-reflected ray through the troposphere ($0 - 12\text{ km}$). Governed by radio horizon geometry, tropospheric refraction ($K$), and rain scatter.

  • Primary mode for coastal VHF marine (156 MHz) [Q10]
  • Rain scattering & absorption severe at 12 GHz [Q20]
  • Diffracts over ridges via Knife-edge diffraction [Q21]
Key Application: FM broadcast (88-108 MHz), Cellular 4G/5G, Satellite, Microwave links.
Characteristic Ground Wave (Surface) Skywave (Ionospheric) Space Wave (Tropospheric / LOS)
Dominant Band [Q6] LF / MF (300 kHz – 3 MHz) HF (3 MHz – 30 MHz) VHF / UHF / SHF (> 30 MHz)
Primary Medium Earth's surface & skin depth Ionosphere (60 – 400 km) Troposphere (0 – 12 km)
If Freq Increases Range decreases sharply [Q8] Penetrates/escapes if $f > \text{MUF}$ [Q23] Absorption & rain scatter rise [Q20]
Terrain Dependence High (favors high $\sigma$ seawater) [Q7] Negligible between reflection points Requires optical & Fresnel clearance [Q36]
Day / Night Behavior Stable day and night [Q9] D-layer absorbs by day, DX open at night [Q1] Subject to ducting via temp inversion [Q16]

Ionospheric Layers & Skywave Mathematics

Critical frequency, Secant Law (MUF), FOT/OWF, and Skip Zone geometry

MUST MEMORIZE 🔴

Atmospheric Ionization Profile

F2 LAYER (~250 – 400 km) Day & Night

Highest altitude & maximum electron density. Reflects highest frequencies (up to ~30 MHz). Responsible for longest single hop (~3000–4000 km, e.g. Manila to Tokyo) [Q2].

F1 LAYER (~140 – 250 km) Daytime Only

Forms during daytime solar ionization; combines with F2 at night to form a single combined F layer (~300 km).

E LAYER (~90 – 140 km) Kennelly-Heaviside

Reflects medium HF signals during daytime. Single hop range up to ~2000 km. Sporadic E ($E_s$) causes anomalous VHF propagation.

D LAYER (~60 – 90 km) Primary Absorber [Q1]

Lowest layer. High collision frequency $\implies$ absorbs HF during daylight. Disappears rapidly at night (enabling nighttime DX skywave) [Q1].

Formula Card: Secant Law & OWF Q17, Q22, Q23, Q24
Maximum Usable Frequency (MUF):
$$MUF = \frac{f_c}{\cos\theta_i} = f_c \sec\theta_i$$
Optimum Working Frequency (OWF/FOT):
$$OWF = 0.85 \times MUF$$

Critical Frequency ($f_c$): Highest frequency reflected back to Earth when launched vertically ($\theta_i = 0^\circ$). Governed by maximum electron density $N_{\max}$: $f_c = 9\sqrt{N_{\max}}$ [Q17].

Incidence Angle ($\theta_i$): Measured from the vertical normal to the ionosphere. If given take-off / elevation angle $\alpha$ from horizontal ground, use $\theta_i = 90^\circ - \alpha$.

If $f > MUF$: Refraction index is insufficient $\implies$ radio wave passes through ionosphere into deep space [Q23].

Optimum Working Frequency: Set at 85% of MUF to provide a 15% buffer against ionospheric height and density fluctuations [Q22].

Direct Board Problem Solution [Q24]:
Given: $f_c = 9.5\text{ MHz}$, $\theta_i = 65^\circ$.
$$MUF = \frac{9.5\text{ MHz}}{\cos(65^\circ)} = \frac{9.5}{0.422618} = \mathbf{22.48\text{ MHz}}$$
$$OWF = 0.85 \times 22.48\text{ MHz} = \mathbf{19.11\text{ MHz}}$$

Skip Distance vs Skip Zone (Dead Zone) [Q11, Q12]

Skip Distance: The minimum distance from the transmitting antenna at which a skywave of a given frequency returns to Earth.

Skip Zone (Quiet/Dead Zone): The annular quiet region between the outer edge of the ground wave coverage and the point where the first skywave returns to the ground. Zero signal is received in this zone!

Board Trap [Q12]: A ham in Pasig transmits 21 MHz HF. Quezon City (10 km away) hears nothing, while Cebu City (570 km away) hears 5/9 clearly. Why? Quezon City lies directly inside the transmitter's skip zone (too far for ground wave, too close for skywave reflection).

Line-of-Sight, K-Factor Refraction & Radio Horizon

Curvature mechanics, K-factor values, Metric vs Imperial horizon formulas

MUST MEMORIZE 🔴
Standard Refraction [Q18]
K = 4/3 ≈ 1.33

Atmospheric density drops with height. Rays bend downward toward Earth. Radio horizon is 1/3 (~15-33%) farther than optical [Q5].

Super-Refraction
K > 4/3 (e.g. K=2)

Strong downward bending. Ray curvature approaches Earth's radius, significantly extending microwave LOS range.

Sub-Refraction
K < 4/3 (e.g. K=0.7)

Rays bend away from Earth. Reduces clearance over obstacles, creating diffraction loss and deep fading.

Tropospheric Ducting [Q16]
K = ∞ (Trapped)

Caused by temperature inversion. RF trapped between thermal air boundaries like a giant natural waveguide.

Radio & Geometrical Horizon Formulas

Watch Units Closely!
METRIC SYSTEM h in meters [m], d in kilometers [km]
Geometrical (Optical) Horizon: $$d_{\text{geo}} = \sqrt{2 R_0 h} \approx 3.57\sqrt{h}$$
Radio Horizon (Standard $K = 4/3$): $$d_{\text{radio}} = \sqrt{2 K R_0 h} = \sqrt{17 h} \approx 4.12\sqrt{h}$$
Total Inter-Tower LOS Range: $$d_{\text{total}} = \sqrt{17 h_t} + \sqrt{17 h_r}$$
IMPERIAL / ENGLISH SYSTEM h in feet [ft], d in statute miles [mi]
Single Antenna Horizon (Standard Refraction): $$d = \sqrt{2 h} \quad [\text{miles}]$$
Total Inter-Tower LOS Range: $$d_{\text{total}} = \sqrt{2 h_t} + \sqrt{2 h_r} \quad [\text{miles}]$$
Trap Alert [Q19, Q32]: If $K=1.0$ is assumed instead of $4/3$, calculated maximum distance is underestimated [Q19]. Doubling $h_t$ increases total range by less than a factor of two [Q32].
DTTB Link [Q30]:
$h_t = 144\text{ m}, h_r = 49\text{ m}$
$d = \sqrt{17(144)} + \sqrt{17(49)}$
$d = 49.48 + 28.86 = \mathbf{78.34\text{ km}}$
If $h_t \to 225\text{ m}$:
$d' = \sqrt{17(225)} + 28.86 = \mathbf{90.71\text{ km}}$
$\Delta d = 90.71 - 78.34 = \mathbf{12.37\text{ km}}$
Maritime Cliff [Q31]:
$h = 50\text{ m}, R_0 = 6,371\text{ km}$
$d_{\text{geo}} = \sqrt{2(6371)(0.05)}$
$d_{\text{geo}} = \sqrt{637.1} = \mathbf{25.24\text{ km}}$
With $K=4/3$:
$d_{\text{radio}} = \sqrt{17(50)} = \sqrt{850}$
$d_{\text{radio}} = \mathbf{29.15\text{ km}}$
Microwave Imperial [Q33]:
$h_t = 600\text{ ft}, h_r = 240\text{ ft}$
$d = \sqrt{2(600)} + \sqrt{2(240)}$
$d = \sqrt{1200} + \sqrt{480}$
$d = 34.64 + 21.91$
$d = \mathbf{56.55\text{ miles}}$

Fresnel Zone Clearance & Knife-Edge Diffraction

First Fresnel zone radius, 60% clearance threshold, and wave cancellation prevention

HIGH-YIELD 🟠

Fresnel Zone Radius Equations

Fundamental Equation ($n$-th Fresnel Zone): $$R_n = \sqrt{\frac{n \lambda d_1 d_2}{d_1 + d_2}}$$
Practical Metric (1st Fresnel Zone, $n=1$, $d$ in km, $f$ in GHz): $$R_1\text{ [m]} = 17.3 \sqrt{\frac{d_1 d_2}{f_{\text{GHz}} \cdot d_{\text{total}}}}$$
Required Clear Clearance [Q36]: $$R_{\text{clear}} \ge 0.6 \times R_1 \quad (60\% \text{ Clearance})$$

Why 60% Clearance? [Q37]: Below 0.6 $R_1$, the diffracted wave path length difference approaches $\lambda/2$ ($180^\circ$ phase shift), producing destructive interference that significantly attenuates the direct ray.

Knife-Edge Diffraction [Q21]: When RF encounters a sharp obstacle (like a mountain ridge), energy bleeds into the shadow zone behind the obstacle. Lower frequencies diffract more than higher frequencies [Q13].

Worked Problem from Notes [Q38] Tagaytay to Calamba Link

$d_{\text{total}} = 20\text{ km}$, $f = 6\text{ GHz}$. Calculate $R_1$ and minimum 60% clearance for obstacle at midpoint ($10\text{ km}$) and off-center ($5\text{ km}$).

Case A: Midpoint ($d_1 = 10\text{ km}, d_2 = 10\text{ km}$)
$$R_1 = 17.3 \sqrt{\frac{10 \times 10}{6 \times 20}} = 17.3 \sqrt{0.8333} = \mathbf{15.8\text{ m}}$$ $$R_{\text{clear}} = 0.6 \times 15.8\text{ m} = \mathbf{9.48\text{ m}}$$
Case B: Off-Center ($d_1 = 5\text{ km}, d_2 = 15\text{ km}$)
$$R_1 = 17.3 \sqrt{\frac{5 \times 15}{6 \times 20}} = 17.3 \sqrt{0.625} = \mathbf{13.68\text{ m}}$$ $$R_{\text{clear}} = 0.6 \times 13.68\text{ m} = \mathbf{8.21\text{ m}}$$
Note: Maximum Fresnel radius always occurs at the link midpoint ($d_1 = d_2$).

Electromagnetic Fields, Power Density & Radiated Power

Power density ($P_D$), Electric field ($E$), EIRP vs ERP, and distance scaling laws

MUST MEMORIZE 🔴
Power Density ($P_D$ or $S$)
$$P_D = \frac{P_t G_t}{4\pi r^2} = \frac{EIRP}{4\pi r^2} \quad [\text{W/m}^2]$$
$$P_D = \frac{E^2}{\eta_0} = \frac{E^2}{377\ \Omega}$$

Decreases inversely with the square of distance ($P_D \propto 1/r^2$) [Q39]. Independent of frequency! At 1 km, 300 MHz and 3 GHz have identical $P_D$ [Q42].

Electric Field Strength ($E$)
$$E = \frac{\sqrt{30 P_t G_t}}{r} = \frac{\sqrt{30 \cdot EIRP}}{r} \quad [\text{V/m}]$$
$$E = \sqrt{377 \cdot P_D}$$

Decreases inversely with distance ($E \propto 1/r$). If distance doubles ($2d$), $E$ drops to half ($1/2$) [Q43]. If EIRP rises by $+6\text{ dB}$, $E$ doubles [Q49].

EIRP vs ERP Conversion
$$EIRP_{\text{dBm}} = ERP_{\text{dBm}} + 2.15\text{ dB}$$
$$EIRP_{\text{Watts}} = 1.64 \times ERP_{\text{Watts}}$$

EIRP is referenced to an isotropic radiator (0 dBi). ERP is referenced to a half-wave dipole (2.15 dBi). Always add 2.15 dB to convert ERP to EIRP [Q50].

Step-by-Step Worked Problems from PDF

Microwave Link Budget Analysis & Path Loss

FSPL formulas, 6 dB/12 dB scaling rules, Fade Margin, and Banahaw-Makiling link

MUST MEMORIZE 🔴
Transmitter $P_t\text{ [dBm]}$
Tx Net Gain $G_t - T_{LL,t}$
Radiated Power EIRP [dBm]
Path Losses $FSPL + L_{\text{atm}} + L_{\text{rain}}$
Rx Net Gain $G_r - T_{LL,r}$
Received Signal RSL [dBm]

Free Space Path Loss (FSPL) Formulas

Frequency in MHz ($d$ in km):
$$FSPL_{\text{dB}} = 32.44 + 20\log_{10}(d_{\text{km}}) + 20\log_{10}(f_{\text{MHz}})$$
Frequency in GHz ($d$ in km):
$$FSPL_{\text{dB}} = 92.44 + 20\log_{10}(d_{\text{km}}) + 20\log_{10}(f_{\text{GHz}})$$
FSPL Golden Scaling Rules:
Double distance only ($2d$): +6 dB loss
Double frequency only ($2f$): +6 dB loss [Q25]
Double BOTH ($2d$ AND $2f$): +12 dB loss [Q26]
$FSPL = (4\pi d / \lambda)^2 \implies$ Inversely proportional to square of wavelength [Q27].

Fade Margin & Worked Examples

Fade Margin ($FM$) Definition [Q34]:
$$FM_{\text{dB}} = RSL_{\text{dBm}} - C_{\text{min, dBm}}$$

The difference between the received carrier signal strength and the receiver threshold sensitivity ($C_{\text{min}}$).

Mt. Banahaw to Mt. Makiling Link [Q29]:
$d = 45\text{ km}$, $f = 6.2\text{ GHz} = 6200\text{ MHz}$.
$FSPL = 32.44 + 20\log_{10}(45) + 20\log_{10}(6200) = \mathbf{141.35\text{ dB}}$
Total Loss $= 141.35 + 0.8\text{ (atm)} + 1.5\text{ (rain)} = \mathbf{143.65\text{ dB}}$
5 GHz @ 100 m [Q28]:
$d = 0.1\text{ km}, f = 5\text{ GHz}$
$FSPL = 92.44 + 20\log(0.1) + 20\log(5)$
$= 92.44 - 20 + 13.98 = \mathbf{86.42\text{ dB}} \approx \mathbf{87\text{ dB}}$
RSL Calculation [Q35]:
$P_t = 30\text{ dBm}$, Loss $= 110\text{ dB}$
$RSL = 30 - 110 = \mathbf{-80\text{ dBm}}$
$FM = 15\text{ dB} \implies C_{\text{min}} = -95\text{ dBm}$

Rectangular Waveguides & Velocity Invariants

Dominant $\text{TE}_{10}$ mode, cutoff frequency $f_c$, phase velocity $v_p$, group velocity $v_g$, and wave impedance $Z_{\text{TE}}$

MUST MEMORIZE 🔴

TE₁₀ Dominant Mode Formula Card

Broad dimension $a > b$
Cutoff Wavelength: $$\lambda_c = 2a$$
Cutoff Frequency ($c = 3 \cdot 10^8$ m/s): $$f_c = \frac{c}{2a}$$
Group Velocity (Signal speed): $$v_g = c \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{f_c}{f}\right)^2} < c$$
Phase Velocity (Wavefront): $$v_p = \frac{c}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{f_c}{f}\right)^2}} > c$$
Velocity Product Invariant [Q54]:
$$v_p \cdot v_g = c^2 = 9 \times 10^{16}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2$$
Characteristic TE Mode Impedance: $$Z_{\text{TE}} = \frac{\eta_0}{\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2}} = \frac{377\ \Omega}{\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2}}$$

Direct Board Calculations [Q56, Q57]

0.65-inch Guide Cutoff [Q56]:
Width $a = 0.65\text{ in} = 0.65 \times 0.0254\text{ m} = \mathbf{0.01651\text{ m}}$
$$f_c = \frac{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}}{2(0.01651\text{ m})} = \mathbf{9.085\text{ GHz}}$$
$$\lambda_c = 2a = 1.30\text{ in} = \mathbf{3.302\text{ cm}}$$
Complete Waveguide Analysis ($a = 2.5\text{ cm}, f = 10\text{ GHz}$) [Q57]:
$f_c = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2(0.025)} = \mathbf{6.0\text{ GHz}} \implies \frac{f_c}{f} = \frac{6}{10} = 0.6$
$\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2} = \sqrt{1 - 0.36} = \sqrt{0.64} = \mathbf{0.80}$
$v_g = (3 \times 10^8)(0.80) = \mathbf{2.4 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}}$
$v_p = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{0.80} = \mathbf{3.75 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}}$
$$Z_{\text{TE}} = \frac{377\ \Omega}{0.80} = \mathbf{471.25\ \Omega}$$
Cutoff Condition [Q55]: Below $f_c$, the wave is evanescent (pure imaginary propagation constant $\beta$, zero real power transferred).

Don't Confuse These! (Board Exam Pitfall Matrix)

Side-by-side differentiators for tricky multiple choice exam questions

⚠️ COMMON TRAP
Power Density ($P_D$) vs Received Power ($P_r$)
$P_D$: $P_t / (4\pi r^2)$ [$\text{W/m}^2$] — No Frequency!
$P_r$: $P_D \cdot A_{\text{eff}} = EIRP \cdot G_r (\lambda / 4\pi d)^2$ [Watts/dBm]

The Trap [Q42]: 300 MHz and 3 GHz at same isotropic power produce the exact same power density at 1 km. Frequency only impacts received power due to effective aperture size!

Critical Freq ($f_c$) vs Max Usable Freq (MUF)
$f_c$: Launched vertically ($\theta = 0^\circ$).
MUF: Launched obliquely ($\theta > 0^\circ$). $MUF = f_c \sec\theta$.

The Trap [Q17, Q23]: Critical frequency is an atmospheric state; MUF depends on the launch angle. MUF is always greater than or equal to $f_c$.

Phase Velocity ($v_p$) vs Group Velocity ($v_g$)
$v_p$: $c / \sqrt{1-(f_c/f)^2} > c$ (Phase wave)
$v_g$: $c \sqrt{1-(f_c/f)^2} < c$ (Information speed)

The Trap [Q54]: $v_p > c$ does not violate Einstein's relativity because monochromatic phase carries zero modulation information. Invariant: $v_p \cdot v_g = c^2$.
