Q1 to Q21 Review

Thyristor & Solid-State Switching Family Tree

SCR UNIDIRECTIONAL

Layers/Terminals: 4-layer (P-N-P-N), 3 pins: A, K, G

Native State: Normally OFF (blocks forward & reverse)

Gate Behavior: Gate turns ON only; loses control immediately after latching!

SCS DUAL-GATE

Layers/Terminals: 4-layer (P-N-P-N), 4 pins: A, K, G_A, G_K

Key Feature: Can be turned ON OR OFF via either gate terminal!

Turn-OFF: Positive pulse to $G_A$ OR Negative pulse to $G_K$.

DIAC BIDIRECTIONAL TRIGGER

Layers/Terminals: 2 terminals ($MT_1, MT_2$), NO GATE terminal

Firing: Begins conducting heavily when voltage reaches $V_{BO}$

Function: Generates symmetric gate pulses to trigger a TRIAC.

TRIAC / QUADRAC AC SWITCH

TRIAC: 2 anti-parallel PNPN switches with single control gate ($MT_1, MT_2, G$).

QUADRAC: Monolithic package internally combining a TRIAC + DIAC.

App: Full-wave AC phase control, light dimmers, AC motor speed.

SCR Turn-ON & Operating Regions

Q1, Q8, Q11
🔴 4 Turn-ON Mechanisms:
  1. Gate Triggering: Normal mode. $\uparrow$ Gate Pulse Magnitude $\longrightarrow$ $\downarrow$ time required to inject charges (faster turn-on).
  2. Forward Breakover ($V_{BO}$ or $V_{FBO}$): Applied voltage exceeds $V_{BO}$ when $I_G = 0$.
  3. $dv/dt$ Triggering: High rate of voltage rise induces junction capacitance current $i = C_j \frac{dv}{dt}$.
  4. Thermal / Temperature: High temp increases leakage to regenerative threshold.
🔵 Operating Regions:

When forward biased below $V_{BO}$ with no gate signal applied, the SCR operates in the Forward Blocking Region (leakage current only).

SCR Failure Mode during High Frequency:

Localized overheating and failure near the gate junction occurs when the rate of anode current rise exceeds the critical $di/dt$ rating. Reason: initial current concentrates near the gate contact before conduction spreads over the full junction area.

Latching, Commutation & Crowbar

Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6
🟠 Latching Independence:

Once triggered, the gate completely loses control. If gate current drops to zero or cut to $\frac{1}{4} I_G$, the anode current $I_A$ remains unchanged (governed purely by supply voltage and external load impedance; e.g. stays 10 A).

🟢 Turning the SCR OFF (Commutation):
  • Anode Current Interruption: Opening the circuit line.
  • Forced Commutation: In DC chopper circuits, an LC resonant or auxiliary circuit forces current to flow in the reverse direction to bring $I_A < I_H$.
Crowbar Overvoltage Protection Circuit:

If an overvoltage spike triggers the gate, the SCR turns ON instantaneously, creating a dead short across the power rail to ground. This quickly blows a fuse or trips a circuit breaker, clamping the voltage and protecting downstream delicate DC electronics. (Bleeder resistor is paired across capacitor).

Relaxation Transistors: UJT vs. PUT

Q13, Q14
Parameter Conventional UJT Programmable UJT (PUT)
Construction Single PN junction on N-type silicon bar 4-layer PNPN thyristor-like device
Terminals 3: Emitter ($E$), Base 1 ($B_1$), Base 2 ($B_2$) 3: Anode ($A$), Cathode ($K$), Gate ($G$)
Intrinsic Standoff Ratio $\eta$ FIXED by internal geometry PROGRAMMABLE externally via $R_1, R_2$
Formula for $\eta$ $\eta = \frac{R_{B1}}{R_{B1} + R_{B2}} = \frac{R_{B1}}{R_{BB}}$ $\eta = \frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2}$, Gate voltage $V_G = \eta V_{BB}$
🔴 MUST MEMORIZE FORMULA Q14 Solved

UJT Peak Triggering / Firing Voltage ($V_P$)

$$V_P = \eta V_{BB} + V_D$$

• $V_P$: Peak firing voltage $[V]$

• $\eta$: Intrinsic standoff ratio ($0.5 - 0.85$)

• $V_{BB}$: Interbase voltage across $B_2 - B_1$ $[V]$

• $V_D$: Forward emitter diode drop ($\approx 0.7\text{ V}$)

Board Exam Example: $V_{BB} = 20\text{ V}$, $\eta = 0.65$, $V_D = 0.7\text{ V}$
$$V_P = (0.65 \times 20\text{ V}) + 0.7\text{ V} = 13 + 0.7 = \mathbf{13.7\text{ V}}$$
⚠️ TRAP: Don't forget to add the $0.7\text{ V}$ diode drop!

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE Q4 Solved

SCR Phase-Controlled Full-Wave Rectifier

$$V_{ave} = \frac{V_m}{\pi} (1 + \cos\alpha) = \frac{V_{rms}\sqrt{2}}{\pi} (1 + \cos\alpha)$$

• $V_m$: Peak AC input voltage $= V_{rms}\sqrt{2}$

• $\alpha$: Firing delay angle (e.g. $90^{\circ}$)

At $\alpha = 90^{\circ}$, $V_{rms} = 220\text{ V}$ (Resistive Load):

$$V_m = 220\sqrt{2} \approx 311.13\text{ V}$$

$$V_{ave} = \frac{311.13}{\pi}(1 + 0) \approx \mathbf{99.03\text{ V} \approx 99\text{ V}}$$

⚠️ TRAP: If problem does not state HW or FW, assume FULL-WAVE!

🟠 HIGH-YIELD Q9 Solved

Thermal Resistance & Max Dissipation

$$\theta_{JA} = \frac{T_J - T_A}{P_D} \implies P_D = \frac{T_J - T_A}{\theta_{JA}}$$

• $T_J$: Max junction temp $[^{\circ}\text{C}]$ (e.g., $120^{\circ}\text{C}$)

• $T_A$: Ambient temp $[^{\circ}\text{C}]$ (e.g., $40^{\circ}\text{C}$)

• $\theta_{JA}$: Thermal resistance $[^{\circ}\text{C/W}]$ (e.g., $1.6^{\circ}\text{C/W}$)

Calculated Internal Dissipation:

$$P_D = \frac{120^{\circ}\text{C} - 40^{\circ}\text{C}}{1.6^{\circ}\text{C/W}} = \frac{80}{1.6} = \mathbf{50\text{ W}}$$

🟠 HIGH-YIELD Q25 Solved

Piezoelectric Generated Output Voltage

$$Q = d \cdot F \quad \text{and} \quad V = \frac{Q}{C}$$

• $d$: Charge sensitivity coefficient $[pC/N]$

• $F$: Applied dynamic force $[N]$

• $C$: Crystal terminal capacitance $[pF]$

Given: $d = 2\text{ pC/N}$, $F = 150\text{ N}$, $C = 600\text{ pF}$

$$V = \frac{(2\text{ pC/N})(150\text{ N})}{600\text{ pF}} = \frac{300\text{ pC}}{600\text{ pF}} = \mathbf{0.5\text{ V}}$$

Note: Matching prefixes cancel ($pC / pF = V$).

$$V_{out} = 10\text{ V} \times \left(\frac{9\text{ k}\Omega}{9\text{ k}\Omega + 1\text{ k}\Omega}\right) = \mathbf{9.0\text{ V}}$$

$$R = \frac{V_S - V_F}{I_F} = \frac{5\text{ V} - 2\text{ V}}{0.02\text{ A}} = \mathbf{150\ \Omega}$$

🔴 MUST MEMORIZE Q46 Solved

PLC ADC Module Quantization Count

$$\text{Dec Value} = \frac{V_{in}}{V_{max}} \times (2^n - 1)$$

• $n$: ADC resolution in bits (e.g., $11\text{ bits}$)

• $V_{in}$: Analog input voltage (e.g., $3.75\text{ V}$)

• $V_{max}$: Full-scale span (e.g., $10\text{ V}$)

11-bit ADC Calculation:

$\text{Max Count} = 2^{11} - 1 = 2048 - 1 = \mathbf{2047}$

$$\text{Dec Value} = \frac{3.75}{10} \times 2047 = 0.375 \times 2047 = \mathbf{767.6} \approx \mathbf{768}$$

⚠️ TRAP: You MUST use $(2^n - 1)$ for ADC digital counts, NOT $2^n$!

🟠 HIGH-YIELD FORMULA Q44 Solved

High-Speed Counter (HSC) Optical Shaft Frequency

$$f_{pulse} = \frac{\text{PPR} \times N_{\text{RPM}}}{60}$$

• PPR: Pulses Per Revolution generated by encoder disk

• $N_{\text{RPM}}$: Shaft rotational speed in revolutions/minute

Given: $\text{PPR} = 1000\text{ pulses/rev}$, $N = 1200\text{ RPM}$:

$$f = \frac{1000 \times 1200}{60} = 1000 \times 20 = \mathbf{20,000\text{ Hz} = 20\text{ kHz}}$$

🟠 HIGH-YIELD FORMULA Q42 Solved

CCTV / VMS Surveillance Video Storage Retention

$$\text{Storage} = \frac{\text{Total Bitrate (bps)}}{8} \times \text{Seconds in Period}$$

• 16 cameras @ $4\text{ Mbps}$ continuous 1080p, 30 days retention:

• Total rate $= 16 \times 4\text{ Mbps} = 64\text{ Mbps} = 8\text{ MB/s}$

• $30\text{ days} = 30 \times 24 \times 3600 = 2,592,000\text{ s}$

$$\text{Total MB} = 8\text{ MB/s} \times 2,592,000\text{ s} = 20,736,000\text{ MB} \approx \mathbf{20.74\text{ TB}}$$

DON'T CONFUSE THESE! — High-Risk Board Exam Distinctions

LATCHING CURRENT ($I_L$) HOLDING CURRENT ($I_H$)
  • • $I_L$: Associated with device TURN-ON. Minimum anode current required to transition from OFF to ON.
  • • $I_H$: Associated with device TURN-OFF. Minimum anode current required to maintain conduction.
  • • Golden Rule: $I_L > I_H$ (typically $I_L \approx 2 \text{ to } 3 \times I_H$).
CONVENTIONAL UJT PROGRAMMABLE UJT (PUT)
  • • UJT: Fixed standoff ratio $\eta$ set internally during wafer fabrication ($R_{B1}, R_{B2}$).
  • • PUT: Standoff ratio $\eta$ programmed externally using two standard voltage-divider resistors ($R_1, R_2$).
  • • PUT Structure: 4 layers ($P-N-P-N$), with Gate tied to internal N-region adjacent to Anode.
DIRECT BANDGAP (GaAs) INDIRECT BANDGAP (Silicon)
  • • Direct Bandgap (GaAs, InP): Direct recombination emits photons (light). Mandatory for LEDs & Lasers!
  • • Indirect Bandgap (Si, Ge): Recombination requires phonon lattice momentum transfer, releasing heat. Ineffective for optical emission.
IRRADIANCE ($E_e$) RADIANT INTENSITY ($I_e$)
  • • Irradiance ($E_e$): Radiant flux incident on a surface per unit AREA ($\text{Watts/m}^2$).
  • • Radiant Intensity ($I_e$): Radiant flux emitted per unit SOLID ANGLE ($\text{Watts/steradian}$).
  • • Radiance ($L_e$): Flux emitted per unit area per unit solid angle ($\text{W/(sr}\cdot\text{m}^2\text{)}$).
